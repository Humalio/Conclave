# Echo sleep hazard

Hazard analysis for **H-08, echo-sleep overdose**. Severity II, likelihood C, risk class 2. Controls owned by Xenobiology (Ayo). Reviewed by the Disclosure Board with an outside physician.

## The hazard

1.1 A keeper who sleeps near the dish while echoes arrive hears the reply in dreams before it arrives, and sleeps badly afterwards. The disturbance is measured in echo-hours (EH); see [[Echo sleep physiology]].

1.2 Above 3 EH per exposure, the volunteer data show disorientation lasting more than 40 minutes in a third of subjects. A keeper who cannot orient cannot run a pulsar fix, and a relay that cannot run a pulsar fix cannot correct a lead-time error.

1.3 Overdose may arise from: a transmission more powerful or longer than planned; a keeper sleeping outside a shielded bunk; signal leak into the quarters (H-02); a corrective listen after a large lead-time error (H-07); or an individual more sensitive than screening detected.

## Controls

2.1 Exposure limit 3 EH per exposure and 9 EH per 30 days, per [[Exposure limit]] (R7).

2.2 Bunks per [[Bunk shielding requirements]]: reclined, shielded on the dish side, eyes-closed visor. A shielded bunk reduces exposure by a factor of 1.4 against an open sleeping place.

2.3 Screening per [[Keeper screening]]; individuals above the 90th sensitivity percentile shall not sleep in the dish bay during the first live listen.

2.4 Countermeasures per [[Countermeasures]]: light schedule, pre-dosing, post-transmission rest.

2.5 The transmission shall be shut down if commanded power is exceeded by more than 0.02 (see [[Shutdown criteria]]).

## Verification

3.1 Predicted shielded-bunk exposure for the planned live listen: 2.1 EH. Margin to limit: 0.9 EH.

3.2 The dose model is fitted to 16 volunteers exposed at low power in the test-stand annex. It is extrapolated to power 0.62.

## Residual risk

4.1 Not yet assessed. The Board shall not accept an extrapolated model as sole evidence (clause 3.4 of the [[Disclosure basis]]).
