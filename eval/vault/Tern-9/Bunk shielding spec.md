# Bunk shielding spec

The station's spec for fitting shielded bunks into the keepers' quarters. Owner: Ottilie. Revision 2, 23 May 2191. Xenobiology owns what the bunks must do ([[Bunk shielding requirements]]); this note owns how we bolt them into a relay that never planned for them.

## Rule one

Where this spec and Bunk shielding requirements disagree, **theirs wins** and we change ours. Ottilie checks the two side by side at every revision. The exposure and posture reasoning lives in [[Echo sleep physiology]]; we don't second-guess it, we just make it fit.

## Where they go

The living volume runs forward of station 38. The feed ring is at stations 41–47. Nobody sleeps aft of station 34; that keeps every bunk more than 6 m from the receiver shroud (BR-10) and inside the screened liner. Every bunk is fitted head forward, away from the dish (BR-8), at the 30° recline Xenobiology chose. Layout:

- **Ops deck, stations 12–18:** 6 bunks (watch keepers).
- **Mess deck, stations 22–30:** 12 bunks in two rows of six, converted from the mess benches. The mess loses its long table. The keepers have been told.
- **Medical bay, station 32:** 2 bunks, one with full monitoring.
- **Instrument deck, station 33:** 2 bunks for the survey team.

## Mounting

1. Bunks mount on the existing 500 mm deck grid with new rail runs. No new holes through the pressure deck.
2. Each rail run is proof-loaded before the bunk goes on. Proof load per bunk: the BR-6 case (9 g forward, 6 g lateral, occupied) times 1.5.
3. Rail fasteners are lock-wired and witness-marked. Inspection records go in the [[Station log]].
4. Each bunk is a screened cell: copper-mesh liner, sealed hatch, no gaps over 2 mm. Xenobiology asked for it so the sleeper hears nothing from the dish.
5. Bunk bases sit on elastomer isolators, which Xenobiology asked for and which we tuned to keep amplifier vibration out of the bunks. Isolator natural frequency 8 Hz.

## Power and data

Each bunk takes 120 W and a medical data line to the medical bay, carrying sleep telemetry and the echo-hour (EH) count for that sleeper. Routing runs in the living-volume trays, separated from transmit power by at least 600 mm and one bulkhead, per [[Cable routing]].

## Installation

Bunks arrive from the vendor in September and go in zone by zone as each part of the screened liner is signed off. Two fitters can do four bunks a shift. Full set: six shifts, plus proof loading. No bunk goes in until Xenobiology has accepted its prototype with volunteers; Dunmore signed up to that. Slot in [[Refit schedule]]: weeks 37–40, starting with the mess benches.
