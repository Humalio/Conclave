# Frost is a contaminant

Position paper. Ayo, station physician, Xenobiology. 14 April 2191. Circulated to The Annex, Signals Lab and the Disclosure Board.

## Position

An unidentified, self-organising material appeared overnight on the cold receiver face of our dish, in a bay that is meant to be clean and cold. It grows. It moves. We do not know what it is, where it came from, or what it does to hardware or to people.

Until we do, Xenobiology treats frost specimen F-3 as a **biological contaminant of unknown hazard**, and recommends that the Echo Case do the same.

## What the evidence shows

1. **It is on the receiver.** The film covered the cold face of tile T-3 ([[IR-0412 Tern-9 cryo alarm]], preliminary findings of 10 April, Finding 1; the report itself was issued on 16 April).
2. **The tiles are pitted, and not only T-3 is touched.** All six tiles, T-1 to T-6, show micro-pits on the cold face that were not in the February acceptance photographs. The pits on T-3 are the densest. The film is thickest on T-3; the other five show nothing to the eye, but Bel's microscope map of 11 April found faint spots on them too.
3. **It came from somewhere.** T-3 sits against dish panel 7, fitted in the 2189 refit (see [[Dish panel 7 (the one nobody ordered)]]). That is not proof. It is a direction to look.
4. **It is active at our operating temperature.** Dormant above 60 K, active below. The receiver cold face runs at 40 K.

## The echo question

If the frost is what rings the dish, every capture we have made on this receiver is a capture of a contaminated instrument, and every timing in [[CAP-014 Air-gapped capture]] is in doubt. A pit is a fault waiting for a signal. I ask The Annex to scrape one tile clean, sterilise the dish and run the capture again, and tell us whether the echo still arrives.

## The echo-sleep question

Our volunteers sat in the annex beside the dish test stand. The specimen was on the receiver face for an unknown period before 9 April. Some volunteers report hearing the reply in a dream, and two pressed the dream button before the receiver registered the echo ([[Echo sleep physiology]]).

I am asking, not asserting: **could some part of what we call echo sleep be exposure to this material, or to something it releases?** If so, the dose model is fitted partly to the wrong cause, and the [[Exposure limit]] is built on it.

## Recommendation

1. Keep the specimen sealed at 35 K; no one opens the cell.
2. Section tiles T-1 and T-3 for pit analysis.
3. Swab the receiver bay, the annex and the cold rack.
4. Once the pit analysis is complete, **incinerate the specimen** at the Corvel Station furnace, 1400 °C, witnessed. Keep the cell camera stills and the log.
5. Enter a hazard for unidentified biological material in the [[Hazard log]].

## Addendum, 19 April

Signals Lab has proposed the opposite: to put a sealed colony in the relay clock loop (see [[Frost as an instrument]]). I disagree in the strongest terms a physician can use in a case document. We would be designing a living, growing thing of unknown origin into a safety-critical timing chain. The note calls the specimen "an instrument". An instrument does not gain three per cent of its mass after a single echo, as the frost did on 18 April. Until we know what it is, it is a hazard, and hazards are not wired into the shutdown chain.
