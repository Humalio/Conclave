# Ethics question - a living sensor

Raised jointly by Petra (Xenobiology) and Imani (Signals Lab), 22 April 2191. Addressed to the Disclosure Board. We are on opposite sides of the frost argument in our own teams, and we agree that the argument is missing a question.

## The question

Signals Lab proposes to put a sealed colony of frost specimen F-3 in the relay clock loop, because it appears to register something about 0.7 s before our instruments do ([[Frost as an instrument]]). Xenobiology proposes to incinerate it ([[Frost is a contaminant]]). Both proposals treat the specimen as a thing: a sensor or a hazard.

We ask instead: **is it right to put a living thing in the clock loop?**

We do not yet know that the frost is alive. We know that it grows, that it organises itself, that it responds before a signal we can measure, and that it gains mass after particular echoes and not after others ([[Frost feeding log]]). Those are the things we would look for in something alive. That is enough to make the question real.

## Three sub-questions

**1. Does it suffer?** We cannot answer this, and we should say so rather than assume no. The specimen ticks in bursts after high-power echoes, and its tick rate doubles. We have been describing that as "activity". It could equally be distress, or feeding, or nothing. A clock loop would expose it to every transmission for the life of the relay. If it responds to transmissions, it would respond hundreds of times.

**2. Who decides?** At present, the specimen's fate is being argued between two technical teams, each with an interest. Signals Lab wants a better clock. Xenobiology wants a clean station. Neither is a neutral party. Nobody has been asked to speak for the specimen, and no process exists for deciding about a living material of unknown nature. We think the Disclosure Board is the closest thing we have to a neutral party, and that it should seek one outside opinion.

**3. What would we be committing to?** If the frost is in the relay loop, the relay's timing depends on it. If it dies, the clock degrades. If it grows, we do not know what the clock does. We would have to keep it alive, which means keeping it fed, which means sending transmissions powerful enough to feed it, which is the one thing the case has decided not to do. The proposal quietly makes the specimen's needs into the relay's needs.

## What we ask

- That no part of the frost be used in any relay article, or incinerated, until the Board has heard this question.
- That the Board set up a short ethics review, with at least one member from outside the Echo Case.
- That the feeding log continue unchanged, and that no one alter the specimen's conditions to provoke a response.

## Our disagreement

Petra thinks the precautionary answer is not to use it. Imani thinks a sealed colony may suffer no more in a clock loop than in a cell on a rack, and that refusing to learn from it is also a choice with consequences. We have written this note together because we trust each other to say that honestly.

## Status

Open. The Disclosure Board must rule on this before any use of the specimen in the relay, in any form, is considered.
