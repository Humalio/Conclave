# Keeper screening

How the four keepers of Tern-9 are assessed before the first live listen. Owner: Petra. Revised 12 May 2191.

## Purpose

Screening has two jobs: to find keepers who would be harmed more than others by echo sleep, and to give each keeper an honest account of what we know and do not know before they consent to take part. The second job matters as much as the first.

## Battery

| Test | What it measures | Exclusion threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Caloric and rotational vestibular tests | Vestibular sensitivity | Asymmetry > 25 % |
| Two-week actigraphy and melatonin onset | Circadian stability | Onset variability > 90 min |
| Shallow annex exposure, p 0.15, 4 s, seated | Individual sensitivity S | S above the 90th percentile |
| Motion tolerance chair | Recovery time | > 20 min to baseline |
| Interview | History of migraine, vertigo, sleep disorder | Clinical judgement |
| Consent session | Understanding of uncertainty | See below |

Keepers above the 90th percentile of sensitivity are not excluded from the station; they are excluded from the **first live listen** only (see [[Exposure limit]]).

## Consent

Each keeper shall meet the station physician and one member of Xenobiology who is not their screener. They shall be told, in plain language:

1. The dose model has no data above p 0.30, and the live listen is at p 0.62.
2. Echo sleep may, rarely, include sensations that we have recorded but cannot explain (see [[Echo sleep physiology]], "What we do not know").
3. They may withdraw at any point up to bunk closure, without giving a reason and without penalty.

Consent is recorded on a form and on video. We ask each keeper to tell back, in their own words, what they have agreed to.
