# The future is answering

*Filed by Vesna, the Annex. Returned once by Halden ("evidence, not adjectives"). Refiled with evidence.*

**Status:** open. Unproven. Not killed.

## The theory

Nothing is wrong with our clocks. The reply is real, and it is early because whatever sends it is not standing where we are standing in time. Something hears what Tern-9 is *about* to say and answers it. We call it an echo because that is the polite word. An echo comes after.

I have logged every one of them for a year. Forty-six before the Authority admitted one. Same lead every time: 0.71 s. A fault drifts. A fault scatters. This does not. It behaves like a fixed distance, measured in the wrong direction.

## Evidence for

- **The lead never moves.** 0.71 s on every echo, at every power above the threshold. See [[Second timestamp]] and [[Lead time vs transmit power]].
- **It answers content, not carrier.** On echo 47 the reply was on the keeper's screen, answering a message that had not left the dish. Read the last line of [[Tern-9 transcript, echo 47]] and tell me that is a stamp error.
- **The frost knows first.** It ticks about 0.7 s before each echo lands, as if it hears the reply on the way in. See [[Frost feeding log]].
- **Echo 52.** A reply logged to a message Tern-9 never sent. If the reply is to the future, then message 52 has not been written yet.

## Evidence against

- No physical mechanism. Halden: "You have a number and a feeling. The number is good."
- [[Firmware 7]] explains the 0.71 s with a register refresh interval, and it is the official line. Until [[CAP-014 Air-gapped capture]] reports, the cheap answer is still standing.
- Echo 52 has no transmission record, which breaks R11. It cannot be used as evidence, only as a reason to look.
