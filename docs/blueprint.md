# Blueprint

The Blueprint is a project’s structured, versioned record of requirements, research, milestones, decisions and papers. The [service](../server/src/blueprint/blueprint.ts), [rights matrix](../server/src/blueprint/rights.ts) and [item schema](../server/src/blueprint/kinds.ts) define its runtime behavior.

The Blueprint replaces the team's **Objective** card and screen (Requirements, Milestones, Status report, Decisions; [dashboard UX](dashboard-ux.md)). It holds what the docs held in [specification.md](specification.md), [acceptance.md](acceptance.md), [decisions.md](decisions.md), [docs/decisions/](decisions/README.md), [roadmap.md](roadmap.md), [risk-register.md](risk-register.md) and [architecture.md](architecture.md), as structured, linked, versioned items the operator and the agents both read.

It serves two kinds of work with one model, without showing either kind fields it does not need:
- **Implementation:** functional and non-functional requirements with acceptance criteria, milestones, releases.
- **Research:** research lines that either **validate** (hypotheses or questions with criteria and evidence, ending confirmed, refuted or inconclusive) or **explore** (questions and findings only).

Either kind may **publish**: a paper frames results as claims, and each claim points at the findings, hypotheses or verified requirements that carry it.

## 1. Sections

| # | Section | Contents | Item kinds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Project Foundation | Executive summary (vision, core objective, value); stakeholders (role, decision rights, seat) | `summary`, `stakeholder` |
| 2 | Product Requirements | 2.1 FRs by module, each with stories and Given/When/Then ACs; 2.2 NFRs by category. Research: lines with hypotheses, questions and findings | `module`, `fr`, `nfr`, `ac`, `line`, `hypothesis`, `question`, `finding` |
| 3 | Technical Decisions & Architecture | Architecture overview (one Markdown body, may hold a mermaid block); decision log, chronological | `architecture`, `decision` |
| 4 | Planning & Execution | 4.1 milestones and roadmap; 4.2 release plan; 4.3 papers, each with its claims | `milestone`, `release`, `paper`, `claim` |
| 5 | Tracking & Governance | Status reports (rolling log); risk and issue register | `report`, `risk`, `issue` |
| 6 | Appendix | Glossary; references (design assets, repositories, API docs) | `term`, `reference` |

A group with no items is hidden (a project without papers shows no 4.3). Every item shows who created it and when, and who last changed it and when.

## 2. Data model

### 2.1 Projects, tracks and parts

- **`projects`** `id` (`prj_…`), `key` (2–8 capitals, e.g. `CNV`, unique), `name`, `kind` (`implementation` | `research`), `status` (`active` | `paused` | `closed`), `review_policy` (`required` | `per_task` | `none`: whether its teams' tasks need review, [MCP guide](mcp.md) "Review policy"; changed with `PATCH /api/projects/:id` or a team's settings, told to the coordinators and audited), `single_team` (0/1, off by default: the project accepts one contributing team, §3), `parent_id` (the ecosystem it sits in, or null, §3), `version` (bumps on every applied change), `created_by_kind/id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`. Report requests are rows per team in `bp_report_requests` (§6). A team names its project in `teams.project_id` (§3).
- A **track** decides which fields appear. It is a `module` item (implementation) or a `line` item (research); a line has a `mode`, `validate` or `explore`.
  - A module shows FRs, NFRs, ACs. A `validate` line shows hypotheses and questions with criteria and a verdict. An `explore` line shows questions and findings only: no criteria, no verdict, no progress bar.
  - **Per line.** an implementation project may add a research line (for example "FTS5 or not"), and a research project may add a module when a line turns into a build. The project kind sets the default view and the card's measures; the track sets the fields.
  - Papers are not tracks: they sit in section 4 beside milestones and releases, in any project, and may cite evidence from any track.
- Lines nest one level (`L2` → `L2.1`). Deeper splits are refused; the coordinator opens a sibling line.
- A team's **part** is the set of tracks and papers it owns (`team_id`, §3).

One table, `bp_items`, with typed fields checked per kind in the service, so new kinds need no migration.

| `reference` | `REF-n` e.g. `REF-2` | `url` or vault/repo path, `kind` (design, repo, api, doc) | — |

`short` on a milestone is a name of up to 24 characters. A paper's page lists the milestones that deliver it (its **Delivers** line) by that name, or by the title cut off when there is none.

`evidence` fields hold outside pointers only (test path, run, share id, vault path). Evidence that is another item is a link (§2.3).

**Outside references** (`external`, every kind; "Outside references" in the console). An item can say which outside records it mirrors: a tracker story, an entry in a team's own decision log, a pull request. The field is a list of at most 20 `{system, id}`:
- `system` is a short lower-case word (`tracker`, `decision-log`, `pr`): a letter, then up to 23 letters, digits or hyphens.
- `id` is the record's id in that system, 1 to 100 characters, kept as written (trimmed).
- The same pair twice is refused. An empty list removes the field.
- **No links from agents.** A write holds only `system` and `id`; any other key (a `url`) is refused. Links come from the project's **outside systems** (§3), where the operator gives each system an optional URL template. When the project lists its systems, a write that names another system is refused with the list. A project with no list accepts any system, and its chips are plain text.
- Search with `bp_read {external: "tracker:90048"}`, or `"tracker:*"` for every item that system tags (§5).
- The JSON and Markdown exports, the restore and the archive carry the field like any other. A restore keeps the references its file holds, even for a system the project no longer lists.
- `stories` on requirements stays free text; it is not moved into `external`.

Keys follow the house style: short, upper case, readable in a task title. The service allocates the next number when the writer gives only the prefix (`FR-TEAM-*`, `PAP-1/C*`). A refused key gets an `invalid_argument` that names the format and the example from this table ("A hypothesis key is H-<line>-nn, e.g. H-L1-02 (or H-L1-* for the next number)."); the examples live in `server/src/blueprint/kinds.ts` and a test checks they match this table. A dropped or deleted key is never reused, so a task ref, decision link or claim never points to a different thing later.

### 2.3 Links

`bp_links(project_id, from_key, to_key, rel, created_by…, created_at)`, unique on (from, to, rel). Allowed pairs:

| rel | from → to | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `verifies` | ac → fr/nfr | implied by `parent_key`, not stored |
| `delivers` | milestone → fr/nfr/hypothesis/paper | what a milestone needs |
| `ships` | release → fr/nfr/milestone | the release scope |
| `governs` | decision → fr/nfr/module/ARCH | the decision shapes this |
| `supersedes` | decision → decision | |
| `threatens` | risk → fr/nfr/milestone/release/paper | |
| `blocks` | issue/question → any | the target waits on it; the target shows one label for both, "Waiting on I4" or "Waiting on Q-TEAM-02", while the issue or question is open |
| `evidence` | finding → question/hypothesis/claim; hypothesis/fr/nfr/ac → claim | the source bears on the target's verdict or support |
| `studies` | paper → hypothesis | planned coverage: the paper sets out to report on this hypothesis. It carries no support: only `evidence` counts toward a claim (§2.4) |
| `refs` | task → any item | stays in `tasks.plan_refs` (keys), no row |

A link to a missing or deleted key is refused on write. The console shows FRs without a milestone as **unplanned**, not as an error, and shows nothing for hypotheses without milestones.

**Task refs to a pending proposal.** `task_create` and `task_update` also take a key that is not in the Blueprint yet but is written (upserted) by an open proposal of the task's own team, such as a new team's first plan, which always waits for the operator. That key is kept apart on the task as pending (`tasks.pending_refs`, `pending_refs` in task views, at most 20 refs in all), and the console shows it on the task as waiting for the operator to apply the plan. When a proposal closes, the hub settles the team's pending refs at once, and nobody has to call anything:
- a key that is now in the Blueprint (the proposal was applied) moves to the task's live refs. The same happens as soon as any applied write adds the key, such as the operator writing the item directly while the proposal is still open: the task's ref goes live at once, and the proposal settles the rest when it closes;
- a key that another open proposal of the team still writes stays pending;
- any other key is dropped (the proposal was rejected, withdrawn or turned stale), and the team's coordinators get one notice that lists the tasks and keys it dropped.

A key that is neither in the Blueprint nor in an open proposal of the same team is refused, as before; another team's proposal does not count. `task_update` with `refs` replaces both lists. A `team_ask`'s `refs` still take live keys only.

The relations live in one table, `RELS` in [kinds.ts](../server/src/blueprint/kinds.ts). Write validation, `bp_read {rules: true}`, the export and the restore read it. The `bp_links.rel` CHECK constraint repeats the list (migration 0122 added `studies`), and the rights matrix gives each relation its rule (§4).

### 2.4 Papers and claims

A paper is the framing of a publication; the manuscript itself stays in the vault or repo (`manuscript`). The hub keeps what agents and the operator must agree on: what the paper claims and what carries each claim.

- **Support** of a claim is computed on read from its `evidence` links, worst first:
  - `contradicted`: a linked hypothesis is `refuted`, an FR/NFR is `dropped`, or an AC is `failing`;
  - `unsupported`: no evidence links;
  - `weak`: links only to undecided evidence (hypothesis `untested`/`testing`/`inconclusive`, finding with `low` confidence, FR/NFR not yet `verified`, AC `open`);
  - `supported`: at least one link to decided evidence (hypothesis `confirmed`, finding `medium`/`high`, FR/NFR `verified`, AC `passing`).
- The **evidence chain** is the claim's links plus one more level: a hypothesis shows the findings linked to it, an FR its ACs. Nothing deeper is walked.
- **Ready to write:** a paper is ready when it has at least one claim that is not `cut` and every such claim is `supported`; a paper with no kept claims is not ready. The card and the paper view show "n of m claims supported" and the claims that hold it back. Moving a paper to `submitted` while it is not ready is the operator's act and lists the claims in the confirmation (decision D1, §12).
- **From result to claim:** a confirmed hypothesis, a medium/high finding or a verified FR has **Add as claim** (console) or one `bp_write` op: it creates a `draft` claim with the item's statement as title and the `evidence` link already set.
- A claim is not a finding: a finding is the team's record of a result, a claim is the paper's wording of it for one venue. One claim may rest on several findings and one finding may carry claims in several papers, which a tag on findings could not express.
- **Papers for a hypothesis** (`papersForHypothesis` in [support.ts](../server/src/blueprint/support.ts)): a paper matches a hypothesis when it `studies` it, or when one of its live claims has that hypothesis as `evidence`. Each paper appears once, with every reason that matched ("Studies H-L1-02", "PAP-1/C2 cites H-L1-02 as evidence"). A planned paper with no claims is found through `studies` alone. `studies` never changes a claim's support or a paper's readiness.
- **Papers for a milestone:** the papers the milestone `delivers`, each with the reason "Delivered by M2".
- **Results and runs of a hypothesis.** A finding belongs to its line. It is a **result** of a hypothesis only through an `evidence` link to it, so the other findings of the same line are not its results. A task is an **experiment run** of a hypothesis only when the team marks it so: the task carries the tag `run` and names the hypothesis in its refs. Other tasks that name the hypothesis stay plain tasks.

### 2.5 History

- `bp_revisions(item_id, rev, snapshot JSON, actor_kind/id, at, change_note)`: one row per applied change to an item, full snapshot. Diff is computed on read.
- `bp_events(project_id, version, actor, at, added[], removed[], changed[], moves[{key,status,was}], note, proposal_id)`: one row per applied write, and what Home's "Work in motion" feed reads.
- Every write carries the project `version` it was based on. Conflicts are per item: the version stays the delta cursor, and a write is refused with `conflict` only when an item it touches (upsert, remove, move) changed after that version, or when an op's optional `rev` differs from the item's current `rev` (`rev: 0`: the key must still be free). Another team's write elsewhere in the project never makes yours conflict. Links and keys ending in `*` are not checked. The error names the keys and carries `current_version` (and `keys`) in its structured data, for MCP and the admin API alike; a version ahead of the project is `conflict` too. Proposals work the same way: they record each item's `rev` when filed (§2.6).
- Support is not stored, so a status move on a finding or hypothesis needs no write to the claims it carries; `bp_events` records the move and readers recompute.

### 2.6 Proposals

`bp_proposals(id, project_id, team_id, agent_id, ops JSON, base_version, reason, status open|applied|rejected|withdrawn|stale, decided_by, decided_at, note)`. A write an agent may not apply directly becomes one proposal (§4). Applying replays the ops against the current version; if any op now conflicts the proposal is marked `stale` and the agent is told to re-propose. At most 5 open proposals per agent, 20 per project. A proposal has a `type`: `ops` (the batch above) or `replan`, the one submission of a re-plan (§4.1), with `replan_id` and no ops.

**Op reasons and partial apply.** Every op may carry `reason` (at most 200 characters; the proposer's text, so L2, never put into a notice) and `group` (at most 40 characters). The operator may apply part of an `ops` proposal (never a re-plan): `POST /api/proposals/:id/apply {ops: [1, 2, 4], note?}`, the op numbers counted from 1. The hub works out what each op needs itself, and returns it as `needs` on an open proposal (per op, the numbers of the ops it needs): the op that adds the parent it sits under, the op that adds a key it links to, an earlier op on the same key (a key ending in `*` counts as written), and every other op of its `group`. The agent's `group` only adds to these. A choice that leaves out an op it needs is refused (`invalid_argument`, naming the numbers). The chosen ops are applied as one batch by the same rules as a whole proposal: only the items they write are checked for having changed since the proposal was filed, and if one changed or an op no longer fits, nothing is written and the proposal turns `stale`. The ops left out are recorded per op (`declined`: number and key) with `applied_ops`, history reads "4 of 5 applied", and the proposer's notice names the ops left out (hub words and keys, plus the operator's note).

**Refine requirements with the coordinator.** The operator asks the coordinator in words to review requirements: `POST /api/teams/:id/plan-request {note, scope: [keys], checks?}`. It is the plan request (`kind: plan`, no new request kind) with `scope` (the keys, frozen at send time; keys that do not exist are dropped; 1 to 1000) and `checks` (`consistency` and `duplicates` on, `missing_ac` off by default). `note` (what should change, required, at most 2000 characters) is the operator's text, so the coordinator's notice (L0) carries it with the scope, the checks and the request id. Asking again while no proposal exists edits the open request; once a proposal exists it is refused (`invalid_state`) and the only way back is **Send back** (a reject with a note), which keeps the request open and clears the link. An ordinary plan request (note only) is unchanged. The coordinator answers with `bp_write {request_id, version, ops}`, and the hub enforces at filing: the write is always one proposal, even where the coordinator could apply directly; only the team's coordinator may use the id; every op has a `reason`; nothing is deleted (a `remove` is refused: a requirement is dropped with an upsert of status `dropped`, and a merge names its target in the reason); an op changes only keys in the scope (items it adds are new); an existing acceptance criterion is never edited, and new ones may be added, under in-scope or new requirements, only when the missing-criteria check was asked for. Nothing is promoted to `agreed` unless the op says so (a new item without a status is a draft). The request is answered when the proposal is applied (in whole or in part); sent back or gone stale, it stays open. A waiting request is For awareness ("Refining requirements"), and its proposal is a Needs you card with Review and Send back. The coordinator is told what to do in that notice (`REFINE_LINE` in [teams.ts](../server/src/teams/teams.ts)): read the scope, run the checks asked for, and file ONE proposal with the request's id and a reason on each op. The coordinator playbook text carries the same line as a separate change, because the shipped playbooks are quoted by earlier migrations. A new requirement (not an acceptance criterion) may be added under any parent, in or outside the scope; the operator sees and ticks it like any change. A withdrawn refine proposal frees the request, so the coordinator can file again.

### 2.7 Writing for the operator

Write for the operator who reads the Blueprint in the console. The body convention and writing warnings apply to the text around the structured item fields.

- **Summary and Records.** The first line of a body that matches `## Records` or `### Records` (any case), outside a fenced code block, splits it (a heading quoted inside ``` or ~~~ stays in its code block): the text before is the **summary**, the text after is **Records** (commit hashes, paths, ledger and run ids, declarations, raw statistics). The console shows the summary and folds Records. A body without the heading is all summary.
- **Codes.** A code is a token in a title or summary that holds a letter and a digit (`A17`, `P26`, `CF-28`), joins letters with an underscore (`H_oa`), or is 1 to 3 letters with a prime (`T′`, `T'`). Not codes: versions (`v0.3`), numbers with a unit, percent or ordinal (`48h`, `95%`, `3rd`), dates, plain words (all-capital words such as CAD included), text in backticks, link targets, URLs, live item keys, the project key and agent ids. A code is **defined** when a live term has it as its title or an alias (primes normalised, all-capital tokens case-insensitive), or when `G-<slug>` exists (`T′` → `G-t-prime`).
- **Terms are the operator's** (§4): a term an agent writes becomes a proposal, so its code shows as not defined until the operator applies it. A write lists such terms as waiting. The operator can also define a code directly in the Appendix (**Define**).
- **Standing.** Papers, claims and hypotheses get one line the hub computes from the support model (§2.4), never from agent text: "3 of 5 claims supported. C4 has only undecided evidence; C5 has none.", "Supported by H-L1-07 (confirmed), from 2 findings.", "Confirmed. Backs C1 and C2, from 2 findings."
- **Next step.** A hypothesis also gets a next step the hub computes from its status and its findings (the ones linked to it as `evidence`), never from agent text. With no finding: "Link a finding as evidence." Untested or testing, with findings but none of medium or high confidence: "Add a finding with medium or high confidence." Untested or testing, with some of them: "Decide its status: 2 of 3 findings have medium or high confidence." (or "both findings", "all 3 findings", "its one finding"). Confirmed or refuted, but backing no claim: "Cite it as evidence in a paper claim." Otherwise there is none.
- **Sources.** The `evidence` field, a paper's `manuscript`, the `source` of a decision, risk, requirement (FR or NFR), issue or question, `[[wikilinks]]` anywhere in the body and paths above its **Records** heading are sources. Paths under Records are repository paths, hashes and ids kept for the record, so they are never resolved and never warn `source_missing`; a `[[wikilink]]` there still counts. Each is resolved per viewer with the vault's one link resolver ([vault](vault.md#links-in-notes)), so a source opens the same note as the same link in the vault viewer. The order is: the path from the note's folder, when the text is in a note (Meetings); the path from the vault root; the path under each of the team's granted folders; the only file in those folders whose path ends with the cited text; for a path cited from another checkout, the only file with that name. `[[wikilinks]]` with a heading or a label, and `%20` for spaces, are read as in Obsidian. A member gets an answer only for files their grant shows; anything else is `missing`, as in the vault API. A source found at another path than cited shows **Found at**.
- **Reading what is cited.** One parser reads every source field, the body and a note's `source` (Meetings), in this order. First, each `[[wikilink]]` is taken whole, commas and brackets included (`[[Notes/Smith, 2020]]`, `[[Meeting (2024)]]`). Second, a text that has the form of a Blueprint key (§2.2, such as `PAP-1/C8` or `FR-TEAM-04/AC2`; a module's bare capitals do not count) is an item, never a file: a key the project has is fine, and an unknown one warns `key_missing`. Third, what is left is split into sources on `;`, new lines and commas. A comma stays inside a name when the text before it has no extension and the text after it, up to `.md`, has no `/`, so `Notes/Smith, 2020.md` is one file while `a.md, b.md` is two. Last, a bracket or quote around a source is trimmed only when it has no partner, so `(see [[Note]])` cites `Note` and `[[A (b) c]]` keeps its brackets.
- **The note an item came from.** When an item's `source` resolves to a vault note, that note is where the item came from: a meeting, a review or a demo. It is resolved per viewer like any source, so a missing note is the `source_missing` warning and never refuses the write. The note's **date** is its frontmatter `date`, else a `YYYY-MM-DD` date in its file name; its **title** is its frontmatter `title`, else the file name without that date and the extension. A note with no date sorts after the dated ones.
- **Questions.** A question to someone outside the team says in `purpose` what the team does today and what each answer would change; the console shows it on the question's row and page, with a soft hint when it is empty. When work waits on an answer, link the question to that work with `blocks`, so it shows under **Blocking work**. A question for the operator is a `team_ask`, never a Blueprint question waiting on them.
- **Unclear requirements.** Mark a requirement unclear rather than guess, with one question to the operator that names it. An agent's `bp_write` that sets `unclear: true` gives that question in `unclear_ask`, in one of two forms, and is refused otherwise (rule `unclear_needs_ask`, whose message says what to pass):
  - **The question itself:** its text, or `{question, options?, recommended?, context?}` as for `team_ask` (at most 300 characters, none or 2 to 6 options). The write opens that team question itself, as the writing agent's, with `refs` naming the requirement, so a new requirement is marked unclear in the same write that adds it. The question's id replaces the text in `unclear_ask`, and the write's result lists it (`asks: [{key, decision_id}]`). A requirement that is already unclear with an open question keeps it: question text sent again (a retry, or a rewrite of the item) is taken as unchanged and opens nothing, so the first question is never left without its requirement. To ask something else, answer or decline the open question first. It counts toward the agent's open questions like any `team_ask`. If the write becomes a proposal, the question opens when the operator applies it, not before (the proposal keeps the text); applying is never refused for the question limit.
  - **An open question's id:** a `team_ask` of the agent's own team, still open, whose `refs` include the requirement, as before.

  Clearing `unclear` clears `unclear_ask` with it, and the operator's own writes are never refused. The operator answers the question with **Clarify** in Needs you (a question's row, routed like any `team_ask`): the answer also clears the mark, in the same step, with the answer as the revision's note and the answerer recorded; the item then shows the answer as its clarification. The operator confirming or overturning an answer the coordinator relayed clears it the same way; declining the question leaves the mark, and the item's row then says the question was declined. A revision made this way names the user who answered. A requirement marked before this rule, with no question, stays as it is: its row offers **Ask the team what's unclear** (a fixed notice to the coordinator), **Edit** and **Drop**.
- **Outside ids.** Tracker story ids, pull request numbers and entries in another tool go in the item's outside references (`external`, §2.2), not in the body or under Records. There they can be searched, and the console links them.
- **Warnings, never refusals.** `bp_write` applies or proposes exactly as before and adds hints for the next write (§5). There are seven:

| Kind | Fires when |
|---|---|
| `undefined_code` | the title has codes that are not defined, leaving out codes a term in the same batch defines |
| `empty_ac` | an AC has no given, when, then or summary, or is titled like "Acceptance" with no `then` |
| `code_first` | the title starts with a code followed by `:` (`CF-28: …`) |
| `final_unsupported` | after the batch, a `final` claim is `unsupported` or `contradicted` |
| `unlinked_evidence` | after the batch, a claim with no evidence shares a rare code (in at most 3 items) in its title or summary with a hypothesis or finding that may back it; one hint per claim, never a link made |
| `source_missing` | a source the write cites is not in the vault |
| `key_missing` | a source field or a code span cites a Blueprint key the project does not have (`PAP-9/C1`) |

The two claim warnings check the claims the write touches and the claims that share a code with a finding or hypothesis it touches.

**The rule agents get** (`BP_WRITING`, `server/src/mcp/writing.ts`, in the `bp_write` description):

> Blueprint items are read by the operator, a person, in the console: write them for that person, not as notes for yourself. Title: what the item is, in words a person knows; no private codes. Body: the first sentence says the requirement, the result or the decision in plain words; for a result, what came out and the number that matters. Put commit hashes, paths, ledger and run ids, declarations and raw statistics under a "## Records" heading at the end of the body; the console folds it. Keep a code only if it is a blueprint key or a glossary term; otherwise say it in words, or propose a term (kind term, key G-&lt;code in lower case&gt;, title the code, body one plain sentence, fields.aliases). Terms need the operator, so put all of them in one write. An acceptance criterion says given, when and then, and its title says what is checked. Cite sources as [[note]] links to vault files. Anything evidence rests on (ledgers, records, registers) lives in the vault; repo paths and commits go under Records. Tracker story ids, pull request numbers and other outside ids go in fields.external as {system, id}, not in the body. A question to someone outside the team says in fields.purpose what the team does today and what each answer would change; when work waits on an answer, link the question to that work with blocks. Ask the operator with team_ask, not with a Blueprint question. Mark a requirement unclear rather than guess, and ask the operator what is unclear with one team_ask that names the requirement; set unclear_ask to that question. Or put the question itself in unclear_ask, as its text or {question, options, recommended}, and the write asks it for you.

## 3. Projects and team parts

Each project owns its Blueprint. Teams can own parts of it.
- Every team belongs to exactly one project. A team gets its project the moment it is created (the admin API or onboarding approval), so `bp_*` work for it at once. A team made with a project name (onboarding) joins the open project of that name, or starts one with that name, so the teams of one onboarded project share it; a team without a project name gets its own single-team project, named after the team; a team without one gets it on first use (a status request or report). The operator moves it into a shared project with `PUT /api/teams/:id/project`.
- **The part opens at approval.** when the operator approves a team (approving its onboarding request, or creating it in the console with `POST /api/teams`), the hub gives it one track named after the team and owned by it, written as the operator: a module in an implementation project (key from the team's initials, 2 to 6 capitals, never a key the project used before) or a `validate` line in a research one (the next `L<n>`). Its coordinator's first plan then applies as drafts inside it, and the operator agrees it in one plan review instead of one large proposal of raw items. A team that already owns a module or line, or whose project is closed, gets nothing new; a team the hub creates without an approval (tests, scripts) opens no part. Code: `openTeamPart` in [blueprint.ts](../server/src/blueprint/blueprint.ts).
- **A project with only one team (FR-PLAN-12):** its whole Blueprint is at project level and the team gets no part, unless the operator says at onboarding that more teams will join (`project.more_teams` on the request, a switch at approval, default no; `more_teams` on `POST /api/teams`). Then the team gets its part at approval as above. A project set to **Single team** (below) is always a one-team project: no second team can join it, so its team gets no part at approval even when the request says more teams will join. While the project has one team:
  - **Rights:** its coordinator has its own-part rights (§4) over the project-level items too, **Coordinator agrees requirements** included; the team agents' rights read the same way. Operator items (summary, stakeholders, terms, opening a track, decisions, agreeing and papers going out) stay the operator's.
  - **Reads:** `bp_read` reads the whole project by default; the team's summary and Blueprint card cover the whole project (scope "whole project"), and the team plan reads every requirement and milestone of the project. The part limit (`items_per_team`, 300 by default) gives way to the project limit.
  - **Tools:** a part option still works (`bp_read` with `scope: part` or `team_id`, `bp_write` with `team_id`, the team's plan and status requests), and its answer adds one plain line: the project has one team, so use the project level.
  - **Second team:** once a second team is approved, the rules for several teams apply: the new team gets its part, and the first team's items stay at project level. The operator gives the first team a part with `PUT /api/teams/:id/project` if it should have one.
  - **Existing projects:** nothing moves by itself. A one-team project that already has a part keeps it, and the rights above apply to its project level. The operator moves a part to project level with `PUT /api/teams/:id/project` and an empty `owns` (for a one-team project, every top-level item of the team goes back to project level; keys, links, ACs and history stay).
  - **Who counts:** every team that names the project and is not stopped, a draft or paused one included; a stopped team no longer makes a new team "second". A team that is done still counts until the operator stops it, so a project whose first team finished is not a one-team project for the next team.
  - **Requirements from onboarding:** the ones the operator keeps at approval go at project level, in a project-level module the same proposal opens ([onboarding](onboarding.md#requirements-from-onboarding)).
  Code: `soleTeam` in [blueprint.ts](../server/src/blueprint/blueprint.ts), `sole` in [rights.ts](../server/src/blueprint/rights.ts).
- A team **owns** the modules, lines and papers given to it (`team_id`, set by the operator or at approval of onboarding). A new module, line or paper written for a team (its `bp_write` or proposal) is that team's by default; the op gives `team_id: null` to leave it at project level. Items inside carry that `team_id`.
- **Inherit:** every agent of the project's teams reads the whole project blueprint. The default read is the team's part plus project-level items, so tokens stay low (`bp_read`, §5); in a project with one team it is the whole project.
- **Keys are per project.** A key such as `M14` names an item of one project; two projects may both have an `M14`. MCP clients keep sending and receiving plain keys in the same call shape, and the hub resolves each key in the caller's project at write time. `bp_*` calls take an optional `project` (key or id): without it, the project is the one of the caller's live teams (running, paused or draft); when those teams span more than one project, the call is refused with `invalid_argument` and the refusal lists those projects' keys. Naming `team_id` names its project too. Ended teams (done, stopped) do not make a call ambiguous, but naming their project still finds them. Code: `locate` in [blueprint.ts](../server/src/blueprint/blueprint.ts).
- **Event-time project:** task history (`task_events`) records the task's project and its blueprint refs when each event is written (`project_id`, `refs` as `[{key, id}]` with the item's immutable id), and a `team_ask` question records its project (`decisions.project_id`), so moving a team to another project later does not move its history. Events written before migration 0119 have no recorded project; readers show them only where the team's current project is the one asked for, and never attribute them to another project.
- **Contribute:** the coordinator writes inside its part directly and proposes anything outside it (§4). A claim may cite evidence from another team's part; linking is a write on the claim, so it needs no rights on the cited item.
- **Single team** (`projects.single_team`, set in Project settings or with `PATCH /api/projects/:id {single_team}`, audited `blueprint.single_team` with from and to). The default allows several teams. When it is on, the project accepts no second contributing team (a team that is not `stopped`): onboarding approval, a team joining by project name and `PUT /api/teams/:id/project` are refused with `conflict`, and nothing is written. The database refuses it too, so two approvals at the same moment cannot both land. Turning it on while two or more teams contribute is refused with the teams named, and nothing changes. Adding agents to the existing team still works. Such a project is a one-team project (above): a new team gets no part at approval, a part it already has stays, and the console shows the Blueprint as one list of project and team items, which is also that team's default `bp_read`.
- **Ecosystem** (`projects.parent_id`, set in Project settings or with `PATCH /api/projects/:id {parent_id}`, audited `blueprint.project_parent` with from and to). It groups independent projects, each with its own Blueprint, under a shared vision. It is one level deep: a project cannot be its own parent, a parent cannot have a parent, and a project with children cannot get one. An ecosystem holds only its Foundation (summary and stakeholders) and the overview of its projects: no teams, tasks, kind or milestones of its own. A project with teams or other items cannot become one, and while it has children it refuses a team and any write outside its Foundation. `GET /api/projects/:id/overview` lists the children the viewer may see, each with its health, the date of its newest report ("No report yet" when there is none), its next milestone and the proposals that wait, read only after the viewer's grant on that child is checked. Parentage gives no permission: grants, task ownership, directive reach and write rights stay per project.
- **Outside systems** (`projects.external_systems`, migration 0134; set in Project settings or with `PATCH /api/projects/:id {external_systems}`, audited `blueprint.external_systems` with from and to). A list of at most 20 `{system, url_template}`, each system named once. A URL template is optional and has at most 500 characters. It starts with `https://` and a fixed host, with no `{id}`, user name or `@` in the host part, and holds `{id}` only in the path or the query, never in the host or after `#`, such as `https://tracker.example/story/{id}`. The hub builds each reference's link from it, with the id URL-encoded, and gives no link when the result would leave the template's host; with no template the chip is plain text. `null` or an empty list clears it: any system is accepted again, and no chip is a link. The project's coordinators get a `project_changed` notice, and `bp_read {rules: true}` names the systems. Changing the list changes no item: a reference to a system no longer listed stays, shows as plain text, and is refused only when a write sets it again.
- **Status meanings** (`projects.status_meanings`, migration 0135, set in Project settings or with `PATCH /api/projects/:id {status_meanings}`, audited `blueprint.status_meanings` with the statuses that changed). The operator may write one sentence, at most 200 characters, for each status of `fr`, `nfr` and `ac`: what "built" or "verified" means in this project (merged, deployed, accepted by the client). The status names stay the same in every project, and the hub enforces nothing: a meaning is text. A PATCH sends only the statuses it changes; an empty sentence removes one. `bp_read {rules: true}` returns the sentences that are set, marked L0 because they are the operator's words (§5), and the console shows a status's meaning as a tip on its pill (§9).
- **Kind.** Changing it keeps every track, item and link, and browse and export find them as before. It changes the landing view, the card's measures and the track a new team's part opens with (§3 "The part opens at approval"). `GET /api/projects/:id/kind-effects?kind=` says what changes before the save; the change is audited `blueprint.project_update` with from, to and who made it. Either kind may publish a paper.
- **Moving a team to another project** (`PUT /api/teams/:id/project`) never releases or reinterprets its work silently. It is refused with `conflict` when the team's part in its current project holds items, it has an open proposal there, or an open task of the team refers to Blueprint keys; the answer's `dependencies` lists them, and nothing changes. A team with none of these moves as before, and editing the part within the same project works as before.

Alternatives the operator did not take: **B. Team-only** (a plan per team; a multi-team programme duplicates requirements per team). **C. Project-only, no parts** (every coordinator writes the whole document and every read costs the whole project).

## 4. Who may edit what

Direct write rights depend on the item, actor, and team scope. An agent write beyond those rights is reviewed as a proposal where permitted.

| Item | Operator | Coordinator (own part) | Coordinator (outside part) | Team agent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Summary, stakeholders, module/line creation, ownership of tracks and papers | edit | propose | propose | read |
| FR, NFR, hypothesis, question: create, edit text | edit | edit while `draft`/`untested`; propose once `agreed` | propose | propose |
| AC: create, edit given/when/then | edit | edit while parent is `draft`; propose after | propose | propose |
| Status and evidence (FR → built, AC → passing, H → confirmed, claim evidence links…) | edit | edit, except the operator's acts in the next row, which are proposals | propose on a **project-level** item (no team part, in a project of several teams; the answer names the proposal and the item stays as it is until the operator applies it); otherwise — (refused: another team's status is its owner's) | propose (evidence only) |
| Agree an FR/NFR (`draft` or `dropped` → `agreed`, `built` or `verified`), take one back (the reverse), accept or reject a decision | edit | propose; agreeing an FR/NFR is edit when the team's **Coordinator agrees requirements** is on (below) | propose | — |
| Finding | edit | edit | propose | edit in a line of its team |
| Paper: create, framing, venue, deadline, authors | edit | edit while `planned`/`drafting` | propose | read |
| Paper → `submitted` and later, drop a paper | edit | propose | propose | — |
| Claim: create, reword, cut | edit | edit while the paper is `planned`/`drafting`; propose after | propose | propose |
| Decision | edit, accept, reject (accepting one that `supersedes` others moves them to `superseded` in the same write) | add as `proposed`; accepted only by the operator or a `team_ask` answer | same | read |
| Milestone, release | edit | edit status; propose dates, scope; a release's creation and text apply when the team's **Release notes** rule is on (below) | propose | read |
| Risk, issue | edit | edit | propose | propose |
| Status report | request one | file with `bp_report` (never as a `bp_write` item) | — | read |
| Outside references only (`external`, §2.2): add, change or remove them, with nothing else in the op | edit | edit, whatever the item's status (an agreed requirement or an accepted decision too); never an archived item | propose | propose |
| Delete (any) | edit | propose | propose | — |
| During a re-plan of the part (§4.1): its marked items (rewrite, re-link, delete) and new items of its kinds | start, cancel, apply or send back | edit; requirements stay `draft` or `dropped`; submit once | — | — |

- Agreeing an FR and submitting a paper are acts only the operator takes, or a proposal the operator applies. The operator is the admin or, for one team's part, that team's decider ([terms](authorization.md#terms)). They make the requirement, and what the project says in public, the operator's.
- **Coordinator agrees requirements** (per team, `coordinator_decides_blueprint`, off by default; the operator turns it on in the team's settings, `PATCH /api/teams/:id`): the team's coordinator agrees an FR or NFR of its own part directly (`draft` or `dropped` → `agreed`, `built` or `verified`, with any edit of its text while it is `draft`). Everything else stays as the table says: taking a requirement back, rewording a settled one, items of other parts or at project level, terms and the other operator items, papers, opening a module or line, decisions, and every member's write. Lockdown and pause refuse it like any write. Each such act:
  - is audited as `blueprint.coordinator_decide` with the coordinator as actor, and its revision note reads "agreed by the coordinator (team setting)";
  - shows in Home's **Work in motion** ("FR-… agreed by the coordinator"; `bp_events.decided` lists the keys);
  - joins one `info` alert per team and day, `bp_coordinator_decided`, that lists every requirement agreed that day ("FR-FLD-07 draft → agreed");
  - can be undone from the item's **History**: **Undo** on that revision, shown while the item still has the status it set, writes the earlier status back as the operator.
  `team_info` shows the setting to the team.
- **Rules** ([ADR-006](decisions/006-blueprint-rules.md), [SPEC-BLUEPRINT-10](specification.md#spec-blueprint-10); table `bp_rules`, migration 0133): the operator lets one team apply a named kind of change without a proposal. The kinds are a fixed list (`BP_RULES` in [kinds.ts](../server/src/blueprint/kinds.ts)); nothing touching keys, rights, goals, the spec or deletions can be one. The list holds one rule, **Release notes** (`release_notes`): in its own part, the team's coordinator creates a `release` and writes its title, body, `notes` and, when it creates it, its `version`. Dates (`target_at`) and scope (`ships` links) still need the operator, and a member still may not write releases.
  - The operator turns a rule on per project and team in the team's settings (**Release notes rule**: Off, On, Paused; `PUT /api/projects/:id/rules`), and can pause it or turn it off at any time. A paused rule applies nothing. A change takes effect on the next write and is audited as `blueprint.rule`. `bp_read {rules: true}` ends with the team's rules and their state.
  - A write that also changes anything outside the rule becomes one proposal, the whole write, like any write with an op that needs the operator.
  - Each write a rule applies names the rule in its history: the event's `rule` (`{rule, keys}`), each item's revision note ("applied by the Release notes rule (team setting)") and the audit event `blueprint.rule_applied`. `bp_write` answers `applied` with `rule`.
  - The operator sees them for awareness: one **For awareness** entry on Home per team, "n changes applied by rules", which opens **Changes applied by rules** (`GET /api/projects/:id/rules/applied`). It counts the changes of the last 30 days since the viewer last dismissed it; dismissing it hides it until a change on a later day, so it shows at most once a day. The list itself keeps every change.
  - A rule gives no operator authority: what it applies stays the agent's writing, L1 to its own team and L2 to other teams, never L0.
- **Superseding on accept:** when a write accepts a decision (`proposed` → `accepted`, the operator's act) that `supersedes` other decisions, the hub moves each of them that is still `proposed` or `accepted` to `superseded` in the same write, with `superseded_by` set and the revision note "superseded by ADR-… (accepted)". A `supersedes` link written in the same write counts. It is not a rule: it is part of the operator's accept.
- **Parents in the same write (staged batch):** the rights check sees the items a write creates before it reaches their children, and resolves parents, link ends and proposal targets in the destination project, including items declared earlier in the same batch. Rights on such a child are computed against its staged parent and the part it inherits. A requirement created in your own module and its criteria, written together, are all inside your part and apply at once; the same batch under another team's module is still a proposal. Link rights see the staged items too, so a `delivers` or `ships` link to a requirement created in the same write is still scope and makes the write a proposal. One refused op refuses the whole write; one op that needs a proposal makes the whole batch one proposal, which is validated again when the operator applies it. Only literal keys count: a child cannot name a parent written with a `*` key in the same write.
- A `team_ask` answer that names item keys in `refs` may create or accept a decision entry automatically (`source: team_ask:<id>`).
- **Questions outside research lines:** a module question (`Q-<module>-nn`) is in its module's team part, as the module's FRs are, and follows the FR row of the table (a coordinator edits it in its own part while it is `open`). A project-level question (`Q-nn`) is in no team's part, so it needs the operator: a coordinator's write of one is a proposal (except in a project with one team, §3), and `team_id` on it is refused. A question's key names its place: `Q-L1-…` sits under a line, `Q-TEAM-…` under the module `TEAM`, `Q-nn` at project level. Moving a question outside a line to `answered` needs `answer`. When a question becomes `answered`, the write result offers one ready-made op (`offers`): a `proposed` decision `ADR-*` whose `context` is the question and its answer and whose `source` is `bp:<question key>`. Nothing is created by itself; the coordinator sends the op with `bp_write` if it wants the entry. When the answer comes through a proposal, the operator's apply sends the proposer one more `bp_proposal` notice naming the answered keys and how to write that op; like every notice it holds hub words and keys only, never the question or answer text, which `bp_read` gives. A key never changes, so a module question cannot move to another module, a line or project level (nor a project-level question into a module): the refusal says to write a new question there (`Q-<module>-*`) and drop the old one.
- Lockdown and team pause refuse writes and proposals; reads stay open.

**Contributor (delegated)**: an agent delegated to a collaborator ([checkout](checkout.md#91-delegate-one-agent)) works in contributor mode. Each row is at most this; never more than the agent's role allows above.

| Item | Contributor agent |
|---|---|
| Everything the delegate's ceiling lets it read: hypotheses, lines, findings, claims, papers, questions, decisions, the team room | read |
| Create a finding, claim, question or risk under an item it may read, in a team's part | as its role allows (a worker proposes a question, claim or risk, as above); the agent is the creator |
| Edit the text or evidence of an item it created during this delegation, while that item is open (`draft`, `untested`, `open`, or a claim of a `planned`/`drafting` paper) | as its role allows |
| Create any other kind; edit an item created by someone else or before the delegation; link from one | propose, by the usual proposal path (§2.6) and its limits |
| Status moves, deleting, moving to another team, project-level items (creating under one too), status reports | deny |

**How the matrix reads in code** ([rights.ts](../server/src/blueprint/rights.ts)):
- Each op gets `apply`, `propose` or `deny`; the worst wins for the batch. An `apply` given only because of Coordinator agrees requirements is marked `decided`, and one given only because of a rule carries the rule (`rule`), so the write records it as above. All `apply` → applied at once; any `propose` → the whole batch is one proposal (checked by a dry run when filed, so a broken op is refused at once); any `deny` → refused with the reasons, nothing written. A `deny` batch is dry-run first too: a bad key, kind, parent or field is refused as `invalid_argument` with its rule before any "Not yours to change", so a writer is never sent after rights for an item no one could write. The `denied` text ends with where the rule lives (this section and `bp_read {rules: true}`). "—" and "read" in the table are `deny`.
- **Contributor mode** (`contributorRight`): applied after the role's verdict, it only turns an `apply` into `propose` or `deny`, never the reverse. "Its own" means created by the agent after the delegation's confirm (`created_by_id`, `created_at`). `bp_read {rules: true}` names the mode, and a refused or proposed op's reason starts with "contributor mode (for <delegate>)".
- **Team agent** is every role but coordinator (worker, reviewer, stakeholder, …). An agent in two teams of one project names `team_id`.
- **Own part:** the item's `team_id` (inherited from its track or paper) is the caller's team, before and after the write. Project-level items (`team_id` null) are outside every part, except in a project with one team, where they count as that team's own (`sole`, §3); a coordinator files a risk or issue in its part by giving `team_id`.
- **Operator acts** (one table, `isOperatorAct` in [kinds.ts](../server/src/blueprint/kinds.ts), read by the matrix and by decider routing): an FR/NFR moving between `draft`/`dropped` and `agreed`/`built`/`verified`, either way (so draft → dropped → agreed is still agreeing, and dropping a built one is taking it back); every status move of a decision; a paper leaving `planned`/`drafting`. Other status moves in the own part apply.
- **Links** take the rights of the item they write on: `evidence` on its target (a claim, hypothesis or question), `delivers`/`ships` are scope (propose), `governs` from a still-`proposed` decision applies, `supersedes` proposes, `threatens`/`blocks` follow the risk, issue or question they come from, `studies` follows the paper (a coordinator applies it on its own part's paper while `planned` or `drafting` and proposes otherwise; a team agent may not write it).
- **Outside references only:** an upsert of an existing item whose only change is its `external` field applies for a coordinator in its own part, whatever the status. It never makes a report writable (reports stay `bp_report`'s), and an archived item refuses every write, this one too. An op that also changes the title, body, status, another field, the parent or the team takes the item's usual rule, with the references counted as part of its text.
- **Modules and lines:** opening one or changing its owner proposes; editing an owned one applies. A team's first track is opened for it when the operator approves the team (§3). Summary, stakeholders, architecture, terms and references propose for a coordinator. Reports go through `bp_report` only: a `bp_write` op that creates, edits, moves or removes a report is `deny` for every agent role, so the cadence, shape and facts of §7 cannot be skipped. Report items are written by `fileReport` (bp_report) and the operator.
- **Proposals** (§2.6) record the revision of every item they write. Applying replays the ops as the operator against the current version; if one of those items changed since, or an op no longer fits, nothing is written and the proposal turns `stale`. At most 5 open per agent and 20 per project (`rate_limited`). The proposer may withdraw an open one.
- **Notices:** a new proposal notifies the team's coordinators (not the proposer) and raises an `info` alert `bp_proposal` for the operator, which closes by itself once the proposal is applied, rejected, withdrawn, replaced or stale; applied, rejected (with the operator's note) and stale notify the proposer and the coordinators. The agent's `reason` (L2) is never put in a notice.
- **Guards:** a write or proposal is refused with `lockdown` (all writes, the agent, or the team locked down), `paused` (team or project paused) or `invalid_state` (team not running, project closed). Withdrawing a proposal is a write too: lockdown refuses it, a pause does not (the agent may still take back what it filed). The service checks the all-writes, agent and team scopes; a PC lockdown is checked by the tool layer, which alone knows the calling machine (for `bp_write` and `bp_report` as write tools, and for a `bp_proposals` withdrawal explicitly, since listing stays open). The operator's writes and proposal decisions are never affected; a decision moves only a proposal that is still `open`, so one that was withdrawn or decided meanwhile is refused and nothing is written.
- **`team_ask`:** `refs` take any live blueprint key of the team's project (task refs too). It takes an optional `project` as the `bp_*` calls do; since `team_id` already fixes the project, a `project` that is not the team's is refused with the team's project named. See §5 and [decisions.md](decisions.md) for `decision: true`.
- **Check mode** (`bp_write {check: true}`): the whole write runs as a dry run and nothing is written or proposed. The answer gives the `outcome` (`apply`, `propose` or `refused`), each op's verdict with its reason, what would be added, changed and removed, the keys a `*` would take, every refused op at once (`problems`), a conflict with its keys and the current version, whether a proposal would hit the open-proposal limit, and the plain-writing hints of §2.7. Lockdown, pause and a stopped team answer `refused` with the same text a write would get. `bp_write` stays a write tool for lockdown, check or not.

Alternatives not taken: **A. Coordinator writes everything directly** (the operator again finds requirements he did not set). **B. Everything an agent writes is a proposal** (the operator becomes the bottleneck for every status move).

### 4.1 Start over (re-plan)

Rebuilding a blueprint used to need one approved proposal per deletion or per change to an agreed item, and deleted keys are never reused. A **re-plan** resets a project, or one team's part, so its coordinator can rebuild it, and the operator reviews the result once.
- **Starting it** (`POST /api/projects/:id/replan {team_id?, kinds?, note?}`, the operator, also from the Blueprint screen): `team_id` names one team's part, none the whole project. `kinds` are what it resets: by default `fr`, `nfr`, `ac`, `milestone`, `release`, `risk`, `term`; `issue`, `hypothesis` and `question` may be added. Decisions, status reports, papers and claims are never reset.
- **Snapshot first:** the export (`export.md`) as it was, kept and downloadable (`GET /api/replans/:id/snapshot.md`), and the rows and links of every item in scope, which a cancel restores.
- **Back to the first status, not deleted:** FR, NFR → `draft`; AC → `open`; milestone, release → `planned`; risk, issue → `open`; hypothesis → `untested`; question → `open`; a glossary term has no status. Every item in scope gets the re-plan's mark (`replan_id`), and keeps its key, links, task references, history and last writer (so the trust of its text does not change). One event ("re-plan … started") goes into history and Work in motion.
- **One open re-plan per part:** a project-wide re-plan excludes any part's, and the reverse; starting one while another is open is `invalid_state`.
- **Notice:** the part's coordinators (every coordinator of the project for a whole-project re-plan) get `replan_requested` (hub text, with the operator's note, L0), naming `bp_read`, `bp_write` and `bp_proposals`.
- **During it:** inside its own part, the coordinator rewrites, re-links (both ends in its part, at least one marked) and deletes the marked items directly, with no proposal per change; new items of the re-plan's kinds in its part join it. Requirements stay `draft` or `dropped`: agreeing them is approving the re-plan. Everything else (kept kinds, unmarked items, other parts) keeps the matrix above. Lockdown and pause still refuse. `bp_read` marks the items `{re-plan}` and says the re-plan is open. Items the operator adds inside the scope join it too.
- **Finishing:** the coordinator submits once (`bp_proposals {action: "submit_replan", reason?}`): one proposal of type `replan`, reviewed as a before/after against the snapshot (added, changed with what changed and the old title and status, removed, links). Submitting again replaces the pending submission. The operator can also file it (`POST /api/replans/:id/submit`).
  - **Apply** (`POST /api/proposals/:id/apply`): FRs and NFRs still in `draft` become `agreed` (keeping their writer), other kinds keep the status the coordinator set, the marks clear and the re-plan closes. If a marked item changed after the submission, nothing is applied: the submission turns `stale` and the re-plan stays open.
  - **Send back** (`…/reject {note}`): restores nothing; the re-plan stays open with the operator's note, and the coordinator revises and submits again.
  - **Cancel** (`POST /api/replans/:id/cancel {note?}`, the operator): every item the re-plan saved gets its status and content back (removed ones return), items it created are removed, links return to what they were, and a pending submission is withdrawn.
- **The docs import's re-plan** (§8, removed in 1.0) was an operator re-plan with no coordinator step; one still open is reviewed as one proposal.

### 4.2 Restore from an export

Rebuilding a Blueprint after a reset used to take many large writes and an approval round per level. A **restore** puts a project's Blueprint, or one part of it, back from its export in one step, which the operator takes and agents can propose. It is the **only** way an export comes back in; the trusted platform-doc import stays its own entry point.
- **Formats.** The JSON export (`GET /api/projects/:id/export.json`, format `conclave-blueprint`, version 1, **admin only**; members keep the Markdown export) carries every item (key, kind, parent, title, body, status, fields, position, team, writers, archived) and every link, so a restore from it loses no content. The writers it names are only a claim (below). The Markdown export (`export.md`) is read as far as it goes: the kind from the key and section, the status, the fields by their type, the body, the links and the architecture text. What Markdown cannot carry is reported in `lost`: the architecture's title, who wrote each item, the team of an item the hub does not know, the position of a new item (it goes last, in file order), line breaks inside a field, and a list value that holds a comma. Another format or version is refused with the version this hub reads.
- **Scope.** The whole project, or one part: `team_id` takes the items of that team (their team in the file for JSON, in the hub for Markdown), `tracks` takes top-level keys, each with everything under it. Both together take the tracks of that team. Items outside the scope stay as they are and are counted in `left_out`. Status reports are never restored: they are the hub's own record (§7). After a reset the teams have new ids: `team_map {file team id: team id}` remaps them explicitly; a file team that is neither mapped nor in the project leaves the item with the team the hub knows, or at project level, and the plan says so.
- **What it does** to each item in scope:
  - missing from the hub: added with its key;
  - deleted earlier: brought back with its key, its history kept (keys are never reused, so a restore is the only way back for a deleted key);
  - different: changed to the file's title, body, status, fields (replaced as a whole, except a field the file does not carry, such as `auto` in Markdown), parent, team and position;
  - the same: untouched, no revision.
  Links of the file are added when both ends exist after the restore; one whose end is missing is listed in `skipped_links` and does not stop the restore. `remove_missing: true` also removes items in scope that the file does not hold, and links between items in scope that it does not hold; by default a restore only adds and changes.
- **Authorship comes only from the hub's records, never from the file (F-23).** Who wrote a text decides how it may be used, so a restore never takes a writer from the file: a JSON field naming an operator, a digest sent with its own file, or the admin clicking Apply proves nothing. A restored item's writer is whoever **introduced** exactly that title, body and fields: the actor of the creation, or of the revision whose previous one held other text (the latest such, if the text came back). A later status move, position change, retag or removal repeats the text under its own actor and proves nothing, so an operator agreeing an agent's requirement never makes its text L0; a revision whose earlier history was collapsed (`revisions_per_item`) cannot show who wrote it, and text an unnamed agent wrote stays L2. The records searched are:
  - the **pre-reset backup** the operator names (`backup`, a file name in the hub's backup folder, `BACKUP_DIR`, default `<DATA_DIR>/backups`), opened read-only and looked up by the file's project key, then the destination's; and
  - the hub's own database, for a restore into a hub that still holds the history.
  A verified operator or import keeps its trust: The operator's vision and agreed requirements stay his text (L0). A verified agent is remapped explicitly with `authors {backup agent id: agent id}`; L1 or L2 is then worked out for each reader from the item's creation and update writers, as for any item. A verified agent with no mapping, and every text with no record, is written under an unnamed agent and reads as **L2** (peer data); the operator's restore cannot vouch for it, but the operator can edit the item afterwards. Whatever the file claims about an unverified item's author is kept as **attribution only** (`attributed_to`, shown in a full read), never as its writer. An agent's restore proposal credits the proposing agent where there is no record. The trust helper gives L0 only to the operator, the import and the hub; any other writer is L2 unless it is an agent of the reader's team.
- **The check plan is the preview.** `check: true` writes nothing and returns the plan: the destination (`project`, `version`, which is the `base_version` to apply against) and the source project; the `digest` of this restore (the hub's hash of the text, the scope, the backup and the maps); the team remap (`teams`: each file team and where its items go); authorship (`authors`: how many items are verified from the backup or the hub, how many read L2, and their keys); `collisions` (a key the hub holds as another kind), `unknown_refs` (a parent neither the file nor the hub holds) and `skipped_links`; what it does (`added`, `restored`, `changed`, `unchanged`, `removed`, `links`, `left_out`, `lost`); the outcome of a dry run, with every problem, including any limit, checked against the whole restore at once; and `prior`, the earlier result if this same restore was already applied.
- **One write, atomic, and a retry is safe.** The operator's restore (`POST /api/projects/:id/restore {text, version, team_id?, tracks?, remove_missing?, backup?, authors?, team_map?, check?, note?}`, admin only) is one write: one event (note "restored from the export", or the operator's), one revision per item it adds, brings back or changes (note `restored`), and no retagging revisions for children that the restore writes itself. It is not held to `ops_per_write`; the item, link, body, field and capacity limits still apply, to the whole restore. If any op is refused, nothing is written and every problem is listed. Applying takes the `version` the check returned: if the Blueprint moved since, it is refused (`conflict`) and nothing is written. Each applied restore is recorded with its digest and base version (`bp_restores`), so sending the same restore again (the same text, scope, backup and maps against the same base version, for example after a lost response) writes nothing and returns the first result with `retried: true`. A restore is refused (`invalid_state`) while a re-plan of the project, or of a team its scope touches, is open: apply or cancel the re-plan first.
- **Agents propose it.** `bp_write {version, restore: {text, team_id?, tracks?, remove_missing?}, reason?}` files the restore as one proposal, whatever the caller's rights (`check: true` returns the plan only). It counts toward the open-proposal limits, is routed to the admin (never to a team's decider), and turns `stale` like any proposal if an item it writes changed meanwhile. Applying it is the same one write as the operator's. Lockdown and pause refuse it like any write.
- **In code.** The parser and the plan are in [restore.ts](../server/src/blueprint/restore.ts); the format constants are the export's own (views.ts). A plan is a list of ordinary ops (parents first, removals deepest first) whose upserts carry a hub-only `restore` marker with the verified writer and the claimed attribution; `bp_write` strips unknown op fields and `POST …/ops` refuses them, so no caller can bring back a deleted key or choose a writer by sending ops. Migration 0120 adds `bp_items.attributed_to` and the `bp_restores` record.

**The team plan** is the team's part of its project. The team page (`GET /api/teams/:id` `plan` and `plan_events`) reads it in plan words:
- requirements are its FRs, NFRs and hypotheses: `draft` reads as open, or unclear with `unclear: true`; `agreed` and `built` read as open; `verified` and `confirmed` as met; `dropped` and `refuted` as dropped;
- milestones are its milestones, each requirement listing the milestones that deliver it; criteria come from the first AC, or `exit` for a milestone; `target_at` is the due date;
- the history is the team's `bp_events`, and the open plan request is the team's open `bp_report_requests` row of kind `plan`.

The coordinator and worker playbooks (both modes) name the `bp_*` tools and the report cadence of §7; the coordinator's adds one line: when a paper exists, link new findings to the claims they carry. Both carry the writing rule of §2.7 (`BP_RULE_COORDINATOR`, `BP_RULE_WORKER` in `server/src/teams/teams.ts`); migration 0102 added it to every copy still at the shipped text, and an edited copy is left as the operator wrote it.

**Writing for the operator (§2.7):**
- `bp_write` never refuses a write for how it reads. When there are hints, a paragraph follows the unchanged first sentence of the result ("For the operator (the write went through; fix these in your next write):", at most 8 lines, then "and n more: bp_read {plain: \"issues\"}"), and `data.warnings` (`{key, kind, tokens?, title, message}`, at most 50) and `data.pending_terms` (`{key, tokens}`, terms waiting for the operator) appear only when non-empty. Proposed writes carry the same.
- `bp_read {plain: "issues"}` lists only the items in the caller's part that do not read plainly yet, each line ending "· not plain: …" with every reason. Without `plain` the output is unchanged.
- `bp_read {plain: "coverage"}` returns only the coverage gaps of the caller's part (§6, Coverage): one heading per list that has gaps, with its count, then at most 20 rows (key, title, status), and one line when there are none. The No task list counts only the caller's own team's tasks. The lists are `data.coverage`.
- `bp_read` with `detail: "full"` returns each paper's, claim's and hypothesis's standing line as `data.standing` (by key), and a hypothesis's next step, when it has one, as `data.next` (by key); its text is unchanged.
- `WRITING` (every agent's server instructions) adds: name agents by their label, not their id.

**In code:**
- `bp_read`'s `since_version` reads `bp_items.changed_version` (the project version an item, or a link at either end, last changed at), because some hub writes record no event. `scope: part|project`, `cursor` and `limit` are additions to the parameters above. Reports are the newest three unless `keys` name others.
- `bp_write` compares every claim's support before and after an applied write and returns `support_changed`.
- `bp_report` keeps its short form as the team's status report too (`team_status_reports`, the Team status screen); the report item carries that row's id.
- `bp_read` takes `q` (every word in key, title or body) and lists items in section order (§1), then position and key. Each item carries `updated_by` and `trust`, from its creator and its last writer, the lower of the two: operator, hub or import L0, an agent of the reader's team L1, any other agent L2 (the admin API reads with no team, so every agent's item is L2 there). A proposal the operator applies keeps its proposing agent as the items' writer (the event records the operator), so applying an agent's text never raises it to L0; the answer's `trust` is the lowest present, and index lines lead with `(L1)`/`(L2)` and end with `· by <who>`. "Unknown kind" and "has no field" list the valid kinds or fields (also in the error's data). `evidence` fields take the reference grammar (F-18, [mcp.md](mcp.md) "References"): every `share:`, `task:` or `/vault/` token with something after the prefix must parse (a bare `task:` in prose is prose), and `bp:<key>` is refused with a pointer to evidence links. Only evidence an op writes is checked, so a value stored before the grammar never blocks a later edit of the item.
- Teams missing the `tool:bp_*` grant get it when the hub starts. `bp_read` and `bp_proposals` are read tools for lockdown; `bp_write` and `bp_report` are writes.

**In code:**
- Every route is behind the admin cookie and CSRF hook, validated strictly (unknown query or body fields are `invalid_argument`, also on `items/:key`, which takes none) and bounded: projects, blueprint items and proposals ≤ 100 per page, history ≤ 50 revisions, attention ≤ 50 items. Leaving `limit` out gives a full page (the blueprint's 100 matches `bp_read`). Lists answer an opaque `next_cursor` and take it back as `cursor`; a cursor from another list is `invalid_argument`. Every answer names the project's id, ready for a per-user scope ([multi-user](multi-user.md)). The import route is in §8.
- `POST …/ops` takes `{version, ops, note?, team_id?}`; `note` is kept on the event (default "operator"), `team_id` names the part the event belongs to.
- `GET …/blueprint` reads the whole project (`bp_read` with `scope: project`); `team` narrows to one part (`team=project` for project-level items), `kind` and `status` take comma lists, `q` matches every word in key, title and body. Keys with a slash (`PAP-1/C3`) are URL-encoded in item paths.
- `PUT /api/teams/:id/project`: `owns` is the team's whole part (top-level modules, lines, papers); its tracks and papers not named go back to project level, children follow (claims follow their paper). A team that moves to another project leaves its old part at project level as history and starts with an empty part in the new one. The new project's version then jumps past both projects' versions and every live item counts as changed at it, so a `since_version` held from the old project returns the whole new view. The team's members get a `project_changed` notice (moved, or their part changed) telling them to read again without `since_version`; pausing or closing a project sends `project_paused` to the members of its teams, resuming or renaming it `project_changed`.
- `POST …/report-request` asks every running team of an active project that has a coordinator, or only the team in `team_id` (a team's decider must name their own team: [multi-user](multi-user.md#4-blueprint-for-members)) (`invalid_state` for a paused or closed project, or when no running team has one). **Each team reports for its own part (F-04, decided, §10):** one `bp_report_requests` row per notified team (`scope: project`, `kind: status`, `note`, `requested_at`, `answered_at`, `answered_by`). The `status_request` notice names the project and the team and carries the report format (`STATUS_FORMAT`, shared with the team-scoped request). A team's `bp_report` answers that team's open status rows only; the other teams still owe theirs. The route records one `review_request` event without a team for Work in motion.
- Team-scoped requests are rows too: `POST /api/teams/:id/status-request` opens `scope: team, kind: status`, `POST /api/teams/:id/plan-request` opens `kind: plan` (with `scope` and `checks` it is a refine request, §2.6), answered by a change to the team's part from its coordinator (an applied `bp_write`, or its proposal once the operator applies it). The team page and its Team status panel read open requests from these rows only, a project-wide one included (`request_scope` on `GET /api/teams/:id/status`).
- `GET …/summary` lists the open requests (`report_requests: [{team, scope, kind, note, requested_at}]`; with `team`, that team's); `project.report_requested_at` is the oldest open one.
- `GET /api/blueprint/attention` (Home): open proposals (oldest first; the list holds at most 50, `counts.proposals` is the true count), escalations (a team's newest report has `escalate`; a later report without it clears it) and contradicted paper claims (computed on read, so one shows the moment its evidence turns). Items carry `label: "paper claim"` and a title "Paper claim PAP-n/Cm contradicted by …"; graph flags carry `label: "note claim contradiction"` or `"stale note"` (F-18), so the console can show both without one word meaning two things.
- `GET …/summary`: measures by project kind, newest report's health with its age (`stale` after 14 days), next milestone, papers with ready state (nearest first), open risks (two, high impact first), what needs the operator; without `team`, one row per team.
- `GET …/export.md` is `text/markdown` as an attachment, headed "generated … edit in the hub"; `?paper=` gives the framing and the kept claims by part of the paper with their support and evidence chain.
- `POST …/restore` takes `text` (up to 1 900 000 characters: either export, told apart by its first character) and answers `{status: checked|applied, plan, result?, retried?}`; applying needs the `version` from the check; a member gets `admin_only`. `backup` is a file name only (letters, digits, `.`, `_`, `-`, ending `.db`) in the backup folder; any other name is refused.
- Limits: `GET`/`PUT /api/settings/blueprint` (API names `items_per_project`, `ops_per_write`, …; `null` resets one) in `system_settings`, loaded at start. Each has a hard ceiling (`LIMIT_SETTINGS` in kinds.ts); `bp_write` and `POST …/ops` schemas take the ceiling (200 ops, 160 000 characters of body), and the service enforces the live limit and names it in the error ("limit ops_per_write, set by the operator"). A lowered limit refuses writes until the blueprint fits. **Retired items do not count** toward `items_per_project`, `items_per_team`, `acs_per_fr` or `claims_per_paper`: dropped requirements, hypotheses, questions, papers and milestones, waived criteria, superseded or rejected decisions, cut claims and cancelled releases (`RETIRED` in kinds.ts). Retired papers do not count toward `papers` either, and archived items count toward none of them. They stay in the part, in reads and in the export. A refusal at a limit names the limit and says how many items are retired. **Capacity is rechecked whenever an item comes back into active work**, not only on creation: a status move out of a retired status (reopening a dropped requirement, restoring a cut claim) and unarchiving are checked against the project, part, parent and per-kind limits like a new item, and a write over a limit is refused whole, so the project is left as it was. This holds for a large rebuild too: a 130-item write with dependent children and more papers than `papers` allows is refused naming `papers`, and nothing of it is written. Status reports do not count toward `items_per_project` or `items_per_team`; `reports_per_project` (default 500, ceiling 5 000) keeps that many newest reports live, plus each team's newest report whatever its age. Older ones get `bp_items.archived = 1` when a report is filed and when the limit is saved (lowering it archives at once; archiving is one-way, raising it later does not bring rows back). Live reads (`bp_read`, `GET …/blueprint`), the card, the summary and Home attention skip archived reports; `export.md` includes them, marked "archived", and counts them in its header. Keys are never reused.
- `GET …/export.json` is the lossless export for a rebuild, and **admin only**: it carries agent and team ids, so a member gets `403 admin_only` even with export access (members keep `export.md`). It is `application/json` as an attachment (`<KEY>-blueprint.json`), takes no query, and is not audited, like the admin's Markdown export: `{format: "conclave-blueprint", version: 1, project: {key, name, kind, version}, generated_at, items: [{key, kind, parent_key, title, body, status, fields, position, team_id, created_by, updated_by, archived?}], links: [{from, to, rel}]}`. Items include archived status reports (`archived: true`); writers are `{kind, id}`, with an operator's console id left out (`id: null`). It is bounded: an export over 1 900 000 characters is refused with `413 too_large` rather than cut short. A restore reads it back (§4.2).
- Work in motion reads `bp_events`; each event carries `project`, and a project-level event has `team.id` null with the project's name. The console shows that name as plain text, folds such rows per project, and says "Status report asked by the operator" for a project report request.

- **Research workspace** (`GET …/research`): `papers` lists every live paper once, by paper ID, with `key`, `title`, `status`, `team_id`, `target_at`, `supported` and `total` claims, `manuscript` and `reasons`. `manuscript` is resolved like a source (§2.7) for the viewer, `{text, where, path?}`: a member who may not read that vault path gets `where: missing` and no path. With `milestone`, only the papers that milestone `delivers`; with `hypothesis`, only the papers §2.4 matches; each row's `reasons` says why it matched. Both together keep the papers that match both. `hypotheses` lists the hypotheses with their line, status, criteria and how many papers study them. A missing key, or one of the wrong kind, is `invalid_argument`. A member needs `blueprint`.
- **A hypothesis's drill-downs** (`research` on `GET …/items/:key`): `papers` as above; `results`, the findings linked to it as `evidence`, with confidence and date; `runs`, its experiment runs (tasks tagged `run` that name it); `changes`, its dated status moves from its own history. Each part keeps its own permission: `runs` needs the `tasks` component and is empty without it, like `tasks`; a manuscript needs read access to its vault path; and nothing here comes from Work in motion, which stays behind `feed`.
- **Writing for the operator (§2.7), additive keys only:** `GET …/items/:key` adds `plain: {summary, records, codes: [{token, term: {key, title, definition} | null}]}`, `standing` (papers, claims, hypotheses), `next` (a hypothesis's next step, when it has one) and `sources: [{text, where: vault|missing|outside, path?, cited?}]`, resolved for the viewer. `GET …/blueprint` adds `plain: {undef: [tokens], empty_ac: true}` to an item only when there is something to say. `GET …/summary` adds `plain: {undefined_codes: [{token, count, keys (at most 5)}] (most used first, at most 50), empty_acs, coverage}` (below).
- **Coverage** (`plain.coverage` of `GET …/summary`, for the same part or project as the rest of the summary): six gap lists, each `{count, items: [{key, kind, title, status, since?}]}` with at most 50 items, computed from what the hub already stores:
  - `no_ac`: live requirements (FRs and NFRs not dropped) with no acceptance criteria; a requirement whose criteria are all waived has criteria;
  - `no_milestone`: agreed or built requirements that no live milestone (not dropped) `delivers`;
  - `no_task`: agreed requirements that no counted task (not in the backlog, cancelled or discarded) names in its refs. Only tasks the viewer may see count: the admin sees every task of the project, a member only with the `tasks` component, an agent only its own team's. For a member without the component the list is `null`, so it never says that hidden tasks exist (SPEC-BLUEPRINT-05);
  - `passing_no_evidence`: criteria marked passing with an empty `evidence` field;
  - `decisions_govern_nothing`: accepted decisions with no `governs` link;
  - `old_questions`: open questions waiting more than 14 days, oldest first, with `since`: `asked_at` when it is set, else when the question was created. This is the one overdue rule (`questionSince` in coverage.ts); the Questions view uses it too.

- **Questions** (`GET /api/projects/:id/questions?team=&status=`, `status` one of `open` (default), `answered`, `dropped`, `all`): the questions of the part or project, filtered like the browse list, with `counts: {open, answered, dropped, all}`. Each row is `{key, title, status, team_id, parent, purpose, asked_to, asked_at, since, overdue, stage, blocks, findings, criteria, line, mode, activity, hint}`: `stage` is `not_asked` (no `asked_to`), `asked` or the status; `blocks` lists the open items it `blocks` (§6.1's meaning of open); `findings` counts the findings linked to it as `evidence`; `criteria` and `mode` come from a validate line; `activity` is the latest of its own update and its newest evidence link; `hint: "blocks nothing"` marks an open module question that blocks nothing while its module has open requirements. `blocking` lists the open **issues** that `block` an open item, in the same shape. Overdue is never set in a research project. Members read it with the `blueprint` component, like Meetings. It adds no kind, status or field beyond `purpose`, and no migration.

  Coverage is a view, not an attention surface: it raises nothing in Needs you or For awareness, sends no notice and changes no other count.
- **Blocking work** (`GET /api/blueprint/attention`, `blocking`): one For awareness row per project and team part with open issues or questions that `block` an open item, "2 items block work", with the blockers' keys and the oldest one's age, opening the Questions view. Only blocking items reach For awareness, never every open question; a row leaves when its blockers are answered, dropped or resolved, or their `blocks` links are removed. It is counted the same on Home, the team page and Blueprint Review. An outside answer is not the operator's decision, so it is never Needs you.

**Limits** (Settings, defaults): 2 000 live items per project; 300 per team part; 50 ACs per FR; 30 lines, one nesting level; 10 live papers per project; 40 claims per paper; body 4 000 chars (architecture 16 000); fields JSON 4 000; title 200; 200 links per item; 50 ops per write; revisions: all kept up to 200 per item, older ones collapsed into one "earlier changes" snapshot; reports: 500 per project kept live (`reports_per_project`, up to 5 000), older ones archived and kept in the export (each team's newest stays live); reports and retired items (dropped, waived, superseded, rejected, cut, cancelled) do not count toward the item limits. Byte usage counts toward team stored-data accounting (roadmap, Operational limits).

### 6.1 Browse, counts and archive

A Blueprint list is the server's, so a count and the list it opens can never disagree (`server/src/blueprint/browse.ts`).

- **`GET …/browse`** answers one page: `{items, total, range, next_cursor, prev_cursor, filters, selection, counts, statuses, facets, gaps, cross, hidden, ignored, project_id}` (`range` is the page's rows counted from 1, `{from: 26, to: 50}`). `filters` is what the server applied (`section`, `kind`, `status`, `sel`, `team`, `q`, `f`, `gap`, `x`, `g`, `s`, `limit`), after it checked and normalised them, so the console shows exactly what it got. `total` counts every matching item, before paging. Rows are in section order (the kinds in their section's order), then position, key and id, so pages never repeat or skip an item. Each row has `key`, `kind`, `title`, `status`, `fields`, `parent` (`{key, title}` or null) and `team` (`{id, name}` or null for project level). `limit` is 1 to 100 (default 25); `cursor` is opaque, as in every list. `q` matches every word in the key, title and body, folded like `bp_read`. `team` is a team of the project or `project` for project-level items. `statuses` lists the stored statuses of the kinds in view that the project holds (an implementation project offers no hypothesis statuses), in each kind's own order; any status of a kind in view is still accepted as a filter.
- **Named selections** (`sel`), each defined once per kind in `SELECTIONS`; the answer names the one it used (`selection: {id, label, rule}`, where `rule` says in words what it holds):
  - `open`: work still in play. Requirements (FR, NFR) draft, agreed or built; acceptance criteria open or failing; hypotheses untested or testing; questions open; decisions proposed; milestones planned or in progress; releases planned; papers planned, drafting or submitted; claims draft; risks and issues open; lines active. A kind with no open statuses (a summary, a term) has no open items.
  - `live` (labelled **Not dropped**, so it never reads like the screen's All): everything that is not retired (`RETIRED` in kinds.ts). This is the total of the Requirements, Acceptance and Milestones bars.
  - `dropped`: the retired items (dropped, waived, superseded, rejected, cut, cancelled), the Dropped screen's list.
  - `month`: items created in the last 30 days (Findings "this month").
  - `decide` (labelled **Waiting on a decision**) and `risk` (labelled **At risk**), **section 2 only** (any other section, or none, refuses them like an unknown selection). Unlike the three above they follow links and read other kinds' states, but they still list only section-2 items, so a count and its list agree (`summary.ts`, one function for both):
    - `decide`: an unclear FR or NFR (waits on **you**); a hypothesis that is testing with a medium or high finding linked as evidence but no verdict, or inconclusive (waits on **the team**; its reason says "Inconclusive: retest, reframe or drop"); an open question that blocks an open item (waits on the person it was asked of, with its age, **Overdue** after 14 days outside research, and the items it blocks); and an FR, NFR or hypothesis that an open **issue** blocks (listed as the blocked item, "Waiting on I4", never as the issue). Rows are sorted waiting-on-you first, then the team, then others, oldest first.
    - `risk`: a live FR, NFR or hypothesis that is not yet verified or decided and has a failing acceptance criterion, a delivering milestone that is late, or an open risk of **high** likelihood **or** high impact that threatens it, **minus what `decide` holds**, so an item is counted once. A milestone is **late** by one function (`milestoneLate`, kinds.ts) that the For awareness missed-milestone row also uses: `missed`, or a target date that has passed while it is planned or in progress.
    - Each row of these two lists carries `why`: the hub's reasons for it, `{code, text, tone}` with fixed wording and only keys, numbers and days in it (a question's `asked_to`, which an agent writes, stays in the row's `fields`). A row also carries `blocks` (the open items a question blocks) and `overdue`. The console's area cards read all of an item's reasons from these two lists, so a blocked item with a failing criterion never looks healthy there.
  - `counts: {all, open, dropped}` counts the same filters without `sel` and `status`, so a selector reading "Open 12" opens those same 12 items over all pages. On section 2 it also carries `decide` and `risk`, counted with the same team and search filters (additive: other sections and older clients are unchanged).
- **Facets and the count table.** `GET …/browse` also answers `facets` (each dimension of the section's kind with its values and their counts under the other filters) and, with `x=<dim>*<dim>`, `cross` (counts per pair of values, `totals`, each row value's items counted once, and `col_totals`, the same per column value). Both come from the same filtered set as the list, before paging. An item with several values of a dimension counts under each. Gaps are named selections.
  - **Asking.** `f=<dim>:<value>,…` filters by dimension values (values of one dimension are either-or, dimensions are all-of; `status:<s>` is the same as `status`), `gap=<id>` picks one gap, and `x=<dim>*<dim>` asks for the count table. A value that no longer applies (no item holds it, or its dimension or gap is out of view or outside the viewer's scope) is left out and listed in `ignored` (`milestone:M9`, `gap:no_task`), the same way for every viewer; an unknown dimension or gap, a malformed pair, or a status no kind in view has is refused.
  - **Dimensions,** from stored values only: `status`, `team` (or `project`), `kind` (when more than one kind is in view); requirements `priority`, `area` (an FR's module, an NFR's category; a criterion takes its requirement's), `milestone` (the live milestones that deliver it) and `task` (has a task or not); hypotheses `line` and `paper` (the papers that study it); findings `line`, `confidence`, `week` (created, ISO week) and `linked` (whether it is evidence for anything); milestones `phase`; risks and issues `likelihood` (risks), `impact`, `owner` (a stakeholder key, has owner or no owner, never a name) and `milestone` (the live milestones a risk threatens or an issue blocks). A multi-valued dimension has a `none` value ("No milestone") for the items without one, and says `multi` when an item counts under more than one value.
  - **Each facet's count** is the list that value opens: the other filters stay, and that dimension's filter becomes the one value. A gap's count keeps every filter and swaps the gap. A cell of `cross` keeps every filter but the two dimensions' own. `counts` (All, Open, Dropped, and section 2's `decide` and `risk`) keeps `f` and `gap` too, so "Open 12" still opens 12; like before, it leaves out the selection and the status.
  - **The gaps** are entries of the named selections, each with its label and rule, and offered where their kinds are in view: No acceptance criteria, No milestone, No task, Passing with no evidence, Decisions governing nothing and Questions older than 14 days are Coverage's own lists (§6, Coverage: the same functions); Unclear (a live requirement marked unclear); Stale (a hypothesis testing whose last linked finding, or the hypothesis itself when none is linked, is more than 30 days old); Linked to nothing (a finding that is evidence for nothing); Late (`milestoneLate`); No owner (an open risk or issue); No change in 30 days (an open item whose `updated_at` is older).
  - **Sort and group.** `s` sorts the list: `risk` (most at risk first: waiting on a decision, then at risk, by how many reasons the section-2 summary gives, then by how many gaps in view the item is in, then a risk's or issue's likelihood and impact, high first), `old` (oldest change first, by `updated_at`) or `name` (by title, then key); without `s` the list keeps the Blueprint's order (and `decide` its own). Ties keep the Blueprint's order, so pages never repeat or skip. `g=<dim>` groups the list by a dimension in view: the rows come group by group, in the dimension's value order with `none` ("No milestone") last, each row carrying `group` (the value it is listed under), and `groups` answers `[{value, label, count}]` for the whole list, not the page. An item with several values is listed under each, so a group's count equals that value's facet count when the dimension has no filter of its own; `total` still counts each item once, and `rows` counts the listed rows that `range` and the cursors page over. `of` is how many items the selection and status hold before the dimension filters and the gap ("Showing 18 of 112"). A group dimension out of view is left out and listed in `ignored` (`g:paper`); an unknown dimension or sort is refused.
  - **Scope.** Counts that read tasks follow what the viewer may read: without task access there is no No task gap and no `task` dimension, and `hidden` says why ("No task needs task access"). Facets and `cross` only name items of the project. A cursor is bound to the filters and the viewer, and refused when either changes. A cursor is bound to every filter but `limit` and `x` (the sort and the group included), so asking for the count table keeps the page. The hub keeps the per-item values for each project version, task state and viewer scope (`server/src/blueprint/facets.ts`), so a browse is one pass over the project.
- **Scope.** A member reads the browse list of a granted project with the `blueprint` component, like `GET …/blueprint`. Archived items are never listed, counted or found.
- **Measures** use the same selections: the Requirements bar is the live FRs **and NFRs** (verified of live), Acceptance the live criteria (passing of live), Milestones the live milestones (reached of live), Hypotheses the live hypotheses (decided of live), Lines all lines (active of all), Findings this month. Open risks are the `open` risks.
- **Saved views.** A user keeps filtered lists as views (`GET` / `PUT /api/blueprint/views`, per user, in the per-user settings, no table): at most 20 per section, pinned first, never in the export, and re-read through the viewer's grants, so a view of a project the user lost drops out. A view holds the filters, the gap, the table axes, the group and the sort; a shared link carries the same words and no more, so the receiver gets counts for their own scope.
- **Item actions.** `GET …/items/:key` adds `actions`, the actions this viewer may take on the item, from the kind table and the rights: `add_as_claim` (a confirmed hypothesis, a medium or high finding, a verified FR or NFR), `mark_submitted` (a planned or drafting paper), `add_evidence` and `cut_claim` (a claim that is not cut), `undo` (`{rev, to}`: a requirement its coordinator agreed under the team setting, back to its previous status), `archive` (a dropped item, §6.1 below) and `unarchive` (an archived one). The admin gets every action the item's state allows; a member gets none today, since each one is the admin's. The console shows only these, and the route that carries each action checks it again (`POST …/ops`, `…/archive`).
- **Archive.** `POST …/items/:key/archive {check?, note?}` (admin) takes a dropped item out of ordinary browse, counts and search, and keeps it whole. It reuses `archived` (the column status reports use, §7): keys, revisions, links and fields stay; the item still opens by its key, marked **Archived**, and its page lists the children archived with it, with their fields (`GET …/items/:key` answers them under `children`); the export lists it marked "archived" with its links. Only a retired item may be archived, and never a status report (their archive is the retention rule, §7). Its live descendants go with it: those that are retired, and those of a kind that has no retired status (a finding, a risk, a term), are archived together; when any is still in play (a kind that can be dropped and is not, or one an open re-plan holds) the archive is refused (`invalid_state`) and the answer lists them in `blocking`, to drop or move first. Each archived item records the key whose archive took it (`archived_with`, migration 0121). `check: true` answers the plan (`keys` that would be archived, `blocking`) and writes nothing. The archive is one write: the project version goes up, each archived item gets one revision (note "archived") and the event names them as changed, with the operator's note. A `bp_read` with `since_version` lists archived keys under `removed`.
- **Unarchive.** `POST …/items/:key/unarchive {check?, note?}` (admin) brings an archived item back, as dropped as before, with the descendants archived with it, each with the status it had (a waived AC stays waived); a child archived on its own before stays archived. A child cannot be brought back while its parent is archived (`invalid_state`, naming the parent): unarchive the parent first. (Items archived before `archived_with` existed come back with their nearest archived ancestor.) Their capacity is checked like a new item's (`checkCapacity`), so a project at its limit refuses the whole change. Archived status reports cannot be brought back.
- **Writes and archived items.** An archived item is kept as it is: a write (`bp_write`, the console, a proposal) may not edit, move or remove it, put an item under it or link to it; that op is refused (`invalid_argument`, "… is archived") until the operator unarchives it. Removing a link that touches it is allowed.
- **Counts with the section.** A section's first page of `GET …/blueprint?section=` carries `browse: {counts, statuses}`, the counts and statuses of its browse list, so the console's filter row needs no request of its own. `GET …/browse` answers `may_archive`, true for the admin only, which shows **Archive** on the Dropped screen.

## 7. Status reports

**Entry** `SR-nnn`: `health` (on track · at risk · off track), `done[]`, `next[]`, `blockers[]`, `escalate` (bool; raises a Home attention item), `refs[]`, `auto` (the hub's facts), author, time, `requested` (the operator's request it answers). The card shows the newest report's health "as of 2 days ago"; older than 14 days it greys out.

**Cadence** (token budget; in the playbook and enforced softly by the hub):
- **Write when something moved, not on a clock:** a milestone reached or missed, health changed, a new blocker or escalation, a paper's deadline within 14 days with claims not supported, the operator asked, or 7 days of activity since the last one. Never more than one per 24 h unless the operator asked or health dropped; a second one is refused with the time of the next allowed one.
- **Shape:** `health` (one word), `done`, `next`, `blockers` ≤ 3 bullets each, each ≤ 140 chars, each a plain sentence for the operator, with the item key where it helps ("Couch layout agreed with Crew Medical (FR-TEAM-04)"). The whole report is ≤ 1 200 chars.
- **The hub fills in the facts** so the coordinator does not restate them: tasks done since the last report, keys whose status moved, claims whose support changed (`PAP-1/C3 contradicted by H-L2-02`), `team_ask` questions still open (`open_team_asks`), the coverage counts of the team's part (`coverage`, each list's count, with the No task list counted from the team's own tasks; §6), and how many open issues and questions of the part block work (`blocking`). The counts are hub facts in `auto`, never in the body's Records, which are the writer's.
- **No report while nothing changed** and the operator did not ask; the hub says so.
- **Reading:** `bp_read {kinds:[report], detail:index}` returns the last 3.
- **How the hub enforces it:** the 24 h window counts reports filed with `bp_report`. The report, its post to the team room and its charge to the key's message limit are written together: if one fails, nothing is filed and a retry is not refused by the window. "The operator asked" is an open status request row of the reporting team (§6); "health dropped" compares with the newest report whose health is known. "Nothing moved" means no task of the team changed, no write touched the team's part, no claim's support changed, health is the same, no new blocker and no escalation. `escalate` raises a `bp_escalation` warning alert and a Home attention item (`GET /api/blueprint/attention`) until the team's newest report stops escalating. An operator report request lets one `bp_report` per asked team through, and each answers only its own team's request. The "7 days of activity" and "paper deadline within 14 days" triggers are in the playbook, not enforced.
- A claim that turns `contradicted` raises a Home attention item at once, without waiting for a report.

## 9. Console

- **Naming:** the feature is "Blueprint". The card's title is the **project name** with a small "Blueprint" label above it and the project key, kind and scope ("this team's part", "whole project" when the project has one team, or "3 teams") beside it. Screens are titled "Blueprint" with the project in the crumb.
- **Card** (team page, replacing Objective; exactly one per team page): the header "Blueprint · <kind>" with the project name under it opens the first section. One line says what waits for the operator, counted from the same list Blueprint Review (Needs me) shows, with the same team scope, so the button always opens the items the line counts: "2 changes wait for you" with **Review** (to Proposals), "3 items need you" or "2 changes and 1 item wait for you" with **Review** (to Needs me); when nothing waits but something is there for awareness, it says what that is ("1 claim not supported", "2 acceptance criteria failing", "3 items for awareness") with **View** (to Needs me); when open proposals wait for someone else, "1 proposal open" with **Open** (to Proposals). The line is left out when there is none of these. When Needs me cannot be read, the line says so ("Could not check what waits for you") and the Needs me screen shows the same with **Try again**, never "Nothing waits for you". Then one list, a single-line row per part, each opening its section. Implementation: Foundation (no count: it opens the project's information), Requirements (bar, verified of live FRs and NFRs; opens the list of live FRs and NFRs), Acceptance (bar, passing of live; opens the list of live ACs), Decisions (count of architecture and decisions), Milestones (bar, reached of live; opens the live milestones), Risks ("2 open", opening those open risks, or "None"), Appendix. Research: Foundation, Lines (active; opens the lines), Hypotheses (bar, decided of live; opens the live hypotheses), Findings (this month; opens the findings of the last 30 days), a **Papers** group with one row per paper ("PAP-1 · 15 Jan", bar of claims supported, or "Ready"), then a **Plan** group: Decisions, Milestones (bar), Risks, Appendix. A bar with nothing to measure (0 of 0) is left out of its row: the row stays, so the section and its first-create action are still one click away. A **Dropped** row (count of retired items) shows when there are some. Every count on the card is the `total` of the list it opens (§6.1), with the same team scope. A team's Foundation, Decisions and Appendix count the project-level items too, since they are the whole project's.
- **Blueprint screen** (`#blueprint/<project>|<tab>|<team>|<status>`; `#objective/<team>`, `#goal/<team>`, `#plan/<team>|…` and the former `#project/<id>` redirect to it): each section, **Needs me** (unclear FRs, failing ACs, claims not supported, and a row to the proposals) and **Proposals** is its own screen. The **Overview page is gone**: the team page's card is the index, and a link to it (`|ov`) opens the team page, or the first section when it names no team. Filters: team (a select on every tab, offered only when two or more teams contribute; the scope stays in the link and on Back), and inside a section one row: a search field, the segmented control **All · n**, **Open · n**, **Dropped · n**, and a **Filters** button whose sheet holds the kind (in the section's own words: Requirements opens FRs and NFRs, Acceptance opens ACs; a research project's section 2 offers Lines, Hypotheses, Questions and Findings and its section 4 Papers) and the status (the exact stored statuses: Agreed, Built, Testing); the button says how many of these are set ("Filters · 2"). The filters live in the route (`#blueprint/<project>|<tab>|<team>|<status>|<filters>`, the last part `q=…&kind=…&sel=…&at=…`, and the list analysis's `f`, `gap`, `x`, `g` and `s`), so Back restores the list and its page. Requirements, Acceptance, Hypotheses, Findings, Milestones and Risks (with issues) add the **list analysis** row, count table and grouped list ([dashboard-ux](dashboard-ux.md), List analysis). A section opens on its **summary view** when it has one, and on **Open** otherwise (today only section 2 has a summary view; sections 4 and 5 open on Open; foundation, decisions and appendix keep their grouped cards, since their kinds have no open state): with no filter the screen shows that, any filter (a search, a segment, a kind, a status, or an explicit `sel=open`) shows the server's paged list (§6.1, 25 a page), with "1–25 of 112" and **Previous** / **Next** under it. A list that comes out empty says so and offers **Clear filters**; an empty section offers the first-create action the viewer may take (**Ask for a plan** for a team's part). There is no separate track filter: section 2 groups its items by module and line, and each track opens as an item. Research titles section 2 **Lines & Hypotheses** and section 4 **Papers & Planning**.
- **Section 2 summary view** (replaces the grouped view "2.1 Functional · by module"; no segment and no route value of its own, the empty route of section 2 is the summary, so Back from a list returns to it). In order: four **counts**, each a button that opens the list it counts (**Waiting on a decision** and **At risk**: `sel=decide` and `sel=risk`; **Verified**: verified of live FRs and NFRs, **Confirmed** in a research project: confirmed of live hypotheses; **Hypotheses decided**: decided of live hypotheses; a count with nothing to measure is left out, never "0 of 0"); the card **Waiting on a decision** (each row says who it waits on, rows waiting on you first, five rows then "Show all n"); the card **At risk** (each row says why: a failing criterion, a milestone overdue or missed, a threatening risk); then one card per area in the Blueprint's own order (module, then NFR category, then project-level questions, then research lines), titled with the module or line title and never its key (the key is the row's secondary text and the header's tooltip), with one bar ("2 of 8 verified"; a validate line "1 of 4 decided"; an explore line shows its open questions and findings instead of a bar). An area opens with the items that need attention (every reason shown), then those in play, and folds its verified items under "n verified · Show"; an area with nothing open starts folded. Folds are a per-viewer convenience kept in the browser (`bp-sum-fold:<project>`), and the page is correct without them. The team page card's Requirements link opens this summary, and its Requirements bar and the summary's Verified count share one total. A member's read view keeps the grouped cards for now.
- **Dropped** (`#blueprint/<project>|dropped|<team>`): the retired items, grouped by the section they come from, a group only when it holds some. Each entry is kept by default; **Archive** beside a row (the admin's only, never inside the row) opens a sheet that lists what goes with it (its dropped children) or what must be resolved first (children still in play), then archives (§6.1). An archived item opens by its key with an **Archived** pill and **Unarchive** in its overflow.
- **Item actions** come from the item's `actions` (§6.1); the console holds no copy of the rights.
- **Outside references** (§2.2): one chip per reference after the item's meta line, "tracker 90048" in the `--t-id` font. A chip whose system has a URL template is a link that opens in a new tab (`rel="noopener noreferrer"`, the full address in its tooltip); any other chip is plain text and not a button. Project settings list the outside systems, each with its URL template.
- **Risk** (`#bp/<project>|RK-n`): likelihood, impact and owner in one muted line under the title ("High likelihood · Medium impact · Owner Saoirse"), then the summary; Records of up to 600 characters are open when the risk opens, longer ones fold under a **Records** control (a `details` element, reachable and toggled by keyboard).
- **Milestone** (`#bp/<project>|Mn`): the title and its description first, then the target date, phase and exit; a **Progress** bar ("1 of 3 tasks done") only when eligible tasks name it, counted like the Tasks card (no backlog, cancelled or discarded tasks) and only from the tasks this viewer may see.
- **Hypothesis** (`#bp/<project>|H-…`): ordered by what a reader wants to know. The head (title, status, one meta line: line, its mode, team, last change) holds the standing line and the hub's **Next** step; then **Statement** (the `statement` field as Markdown, the summary under it), **Criteria** (one Markdown block), **Results** (the `evidence` note, then the findings linked as evidence, newest first, with confidence and date), **Papers**, **Experiment runs** and **Status changes**; Links, Tasks and History on the side; Records last, always folded. Any other field an agent wrote is listed after Status changes.
- **FR detail** (`#bp/<project>/<key>`): title, status, priority, module, stories, ACs as Given/When/Then rows with status pills and evidence, links (milestones, release, decisions, risks, claims it carries, tasks with done counts), authorship, history.
- **Paper** (`#bp/<project>/PAP-n`): framing (question, contribution, abstract); venue, type, deadline, status, authors, manuscript link, the milestone that delivers it; a **readiness** card (n of m claims supported, one coloured segment per kept claim); claims as an outline by part of the paper, one row each with its first piece of evidence and its support pill (the claim screen is a verdict with its evidence as rows: claim ← hypothesis with verdict ← findings with confidence, or ← FR with verified ACs; **Add evidence** in the app bar and, in the overflow, **Cut claim**). **Mark submitted** is the one app-bar action; Export outline is in the overflow. Confirmed hypotheses and findings elsewhere offer **Add as claim**.
- **Writing for the operator (§2.7):** every item shows the hub's standing line first (papers, claims, hypotheses), then its summary; Records fold closed under it, on claims too. A **Sources** row lists each source: one in the vault opens in the vault viewer, with **Found at** when it was found at another path; one that is not shows "Not in the vault". Codes in the title, summary and given/when/then are marked: a defined code (dotted accent underline) shows its term and opens it, an undefined one (dashed warn underline) says it is not in the glossary. Agent ids in agent text show as the agent's label. One **Check · n** pill on the item lists every hint in the words of its warning; list rows add "2 undefined codes" or "Nothing to check" to their meta, and the Acceptance criteria card adds "n empty". The Appendix's glossary rows show each term's definition and aliases, and a **Not defined · n** group lists the codes in use with no term, each with **Define** (a term written as operator).
- **From and Meetings:** an item whose `source` resolves to a vault note shows **From:** with the note's title and date (left out when the note has none) under its meta line, opening the note in the vault viewer (the path in its tooltip). The Blueprint's **Meetings** view (`#blueprint/<project>|mtg|<team>`, beside the sections; the card's last row, **Meetings**, opens it) lists those notes newest first, each with its date, its path and the items that came from it, every row opening its item. A note this viewer can't read is left out, and its items are not counted. With none, it says so in one line.
- **Decision entry**: context, options (chosen marked), decision and date, decided by (operator, or "via question dec_…"), consequences, governs, supersedes. A decision made from an answered Blueprint question (`source: bp:Q-…`) shows "From Q-…", which opens the question.
- **Waiting on**: an item that an open issue or question `blocks` shows "Waiting on I4" or "Waiting on Q-TEAM-02" under its kind line, each key opening its item; its Links list reads "waiting on" for the blocker, and the blocker's reads "waiting on this". A question shows Asked to, Asked and Answer; a module's questions are listed under its requirements in section 2, and project-level questions in their own group.
- **Report entry**: health pill, done / next / blockers, the hub's facts in a muted line, the request it answers.
- **Research variant**: lines as the second level of section 2; a validate line shows its hypotheses with verdict pills; an explore line shows questions and findings, no verdict, no progress bar.
- **Research workspace** (`#blueprint/<project>|res|<team>|<filter>`): the first page of a research project. The card's header and a Blueprint link that names no section open it. Two cards: **Papers** (planned and published alike, each row with status, deadline, claims supported and the manuscript when the viewer may read it) and **Hypotheses** (status and criteria, each opening its page). Above them, a **Milestone** and a **Hypothesis** filter (`m:<key>` or `h:<key>` in the filter slot); a filtered paper row says why it matches. An implementation project keeps Project Foundation as its first page.
- **Hypothesis page**: status, statement and criteria, then **Papers** (each with its reason), **Results** (findings linked as evidence, with confidence and date), **Experiment runs** (tasks tagged `run` that name it, shown only with task access) and **Status changes** (dated, from its history). A finding of the same line without that link is not listed.
- **Questions** (`#blueprint/<project>|q|<team>|<status>`, beside Coverage and Meetings; the card's **Questions** row opens it; the row reads "2 items block work" when something blocks work, else how many questions are open; the summary carries it as `questions: {open, blocking}`): the open questions by default, with Open · Answered · Dropped · All. Every question row shows its `purpose`, or the soft hint "No purpose yet: say what we do now, and what each answer changes." In a research project the status row (Open, Answered, Dropped, All) has a **Line** dropdown on its right instead of a second row of buttons: the button reads "Line: L1" ("Line: All", "Line: Project"), and its menu lists All lines, each line by code and a short title (cut with an ellipsis, the full title on hover) and Project level, each with its count. The choice stays in the route as `l=<line>`, so links, reload and Back keep it; on phones the dropdown wraps under the status row, aligned right.
  - **Software projects** read it as blockers, in three groups, each row once, in the first group it fits: **Blocking work** (open issues and questions that `block` an open item: what waits, and for how long), **Waiting on someone** (a stakeholder in `asked_to`: one block per stakeholder with **Copy as one message**, each row with when it was asked, "date not recorded" without `asked_at`, and **Overdue** past 14 days by the shared rule) and **Not asked yet** (no `asked_to`). A stakeholder's message also carries their questions from Blocking work, and a stakeholder whose questions all block work still gets a block ("0 here · Q-1 above") and its button. A muted "Blocks nothing yet" hint marks a module question with open requirements and no `blocks` link; it is never counted.
  - **Copy as one message** copies plain text, the questions and their purposes without Records, and sends nothing. Without a clipboard, a sheet shows the text in a box to select.
  - **Research projects** read it as one flat list, newest activity first, filtered by line: each row has its line and mode, the findings count and, on a validate line, the start of its criteria. No overdue marker and no verdict.
- **Question page:** a step line from the fields, with no status of its own: Not asked → Asked <stakeholder> on <date> (or "date not recorded") → Answered or Dropped; under a research line, Open → Answered by n findings. Then the purpose, and **Decided in**: every decision whose `source` is `bp:<question key>`, each with its status ("Proposed: ADR-014", "Decided in ADR-012"; a superseded one names its successor).
- **Coverage** (`#blueprint/<project>|cov|<team>`, from the card's **Coverage** row): one card per gap list that has gaps, each with its name and count ("No acceptance criteria", "No milestone", "No task", "Passing with no evidence", "Decisions governing nothing", "Questions older than 14 days"); every row opens its item. A card shows its first 8 rows and **Show all n** for the rest; two columns from 1180 px (one list takes the full width), one below. A muted line above says "n gaps in k lists · counts the tasks you can see". With no gaps, one line: "No gaps: every requirement has criteria, a milestone and a task, and nothing is waiting." A member without task access sees no No task card and the line says so.
- **Status meanings** (§3): where the operator wrote a meaning, an FR's, NFR's or AC's status pill on its item page sits in a focusable wrapper (`.ref` pattern: hover or focus shows the tip "**Built** in this project: <sentence>", with "Set by the operator in project settings" under it; on touch, a tap opens it as a sheet). The pill itself is not a button, and a status with no meaning draws as before.
- **Proposals**: a "Proposals" screen in the Blueprint, an attention item on Home, and a row in the proposing team's **Needs attention** (its reason, "Blueprint change · <agent> · <age>", **Apply**, or **Review** for a submitted start over, which is never applied without its diff); each shows the diff and **Apply** / **Reject**.
- **Projects** (`#projects`, **Projects** on the Teams header and in its phone overflow): every project grouped Active, Paused, Closed, each row with key, kind, teams, items and keywords. **New project** creates a standalone one (name, a key of 2 to 8 capital letters suggested from the name, kind, keywords with Research & Development ticked; `POST /api/projects`). A row opens **Edit project**: name, kind, status, **Single team**, **Ecosystem** and keywords (`PATCH /api/projects/:id`); closing asks once more, and a change of kind on a populated project first shows what it changes and keeps. Beside the list, **Keywords** lists the known keywords with how many projects and teams have each, and **Edit** adds, reorders and removes them (`GET` / `PUT /api/settings/keywords`, [specification](specification.md#keywords-on-projects-and-teams)); then **Blueprint limits** shows each limit and, when changed, its default; **Edit** opens the limits with their default and maximum, a **Reset** per limit and **Reset all to defaults** (`GET` / `PUT /api/settings/blueprint`).
- **Older revisions**: an item's History shows the newest ten; **Show older revisions** pages back through `GET /api/projects/:id/items/:key/history` (opaque cursor, 50 a page).
- **Past start-overs**: a team's **Recruits and start-overs** row (TEAM card) opens `#thist/<team>`: its recruit requests (All, Waiting, Approved, Denied) and the re-plans of its project for its part or the whole project. An open one goes to the Blueprint; an applied or cancelled one opens `#replan/<id>`: the diff against its snapshot (added, changed, removed, links, unchanged count), its scope, kinds, dates, notes and the snapshot download.
