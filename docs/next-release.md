# Conclave implementation brief

**Historical design record, 17 November 2026.** Version 5 adds the owner cap on work passed between agents, the claim step before owner enforcement goes live, one restore path with authorship from the backup, project-qualified keys, a teamless vault setup, one word for ecosystems, an attention glossary, and a simpler Hardware release. This brief supersedes proposals 2 and 3 as a design recommendation. The requirements are the 35 target `SPEC-*` entries in the working [system specification](specification.md#target-platform-requirements). Promote each accepted behavior into the relevant subsystem guide and acceptance checklist when it ships.

For the subsequent review of the implemented workflows as of 18 November 2026, see the [workflow repair package](workflow-repairs.md). It proposes a focused maintenance batch with acceptance cases.

## Product decision

Give the operator one place to open a project, one place to answer the work waiting for him, and a direct path from either to the underlying record. Give a collaborator the same project, expressed through that person's access. A collaborator invited only to share notes should be able to land in the vault, connect an agent, and edit an authorized note without creating a project.

The operator confirmed the four product questions in proposal 3: **Who is connected? What may they do? What have they done? What needs me now?** The operator also confirmed collaborator-owned agents capped by their owner's grant, a full reset before the new releases ship, and Hardware as the last delivery. The hub continues to coordinate sessions that run on other machines. Presence comes from a login or heartbeat, progress from a task event, review from a submitted version, and machine use from a reservation reported by its holder. Keep those distinctions visible.

The following decisions resolve gaps in proposal 3.

| Decision for implementation | Why it matters to the operator |
|---|---|
| Preserve the explicit single-team choice in Project settings. Derive the scope picker from the choices actually available. | “Keep this project single-team” must survive the next onboarding request. Hiding a picker alone cannot honor that choice. |
| Give planned papers a direct, typed link to the hypotheses they study. Keep the existing evidence links and milestone deliveries. | A researcher can find two planned papers about a hypothesis before either has claims or results. Later they can see which paper actually has supporting evidence. |
| Make agent delegation explicit inside a person's grant. Establish provenance separately when importing Blueprint content. | Seeing a project does not authorize an agent to write to it. Giving the operator an import to approve must not let a file declare its own text to be the operator's instructions. |

## 1. Project, team and Blueprint

A project is one body of work with one Blueprint. A team contributes through its existing part. Modules and research lines organize content. Project-level items, team write rights, proposal routing and team deciders retain their existing meanings. A part can contain several tracks. A single team sees shared project items and its part in one list. Creating a requirement there uses its team's module. Editing the project vision still follows the project-level approval rule. [Blueprint contract](blueprint.md) · [Write rights](../server/src/blueprint/rights.ts)

**Foundation** in the Blueprint opens project information and carries no count. The project name and kind identify the Blueprint and lead back to its overview. The project page shows vision, teams, milestones, and child projects. The operator can edit kind, single-team policy, intake, review policy and other project-wide settings there. A team settings link may open the relevant project setting. Use the existing member project route and admin navigation tree, with shared sections and server-scoped payloads. Preserve old deep links, Back behavior and the distinct member shell.

* **Team scope.** Default to allowing several teams. Hide the selector for zero or one contributing team. With multiple teams, offer the project and the relevant team parts. Show the scope on deep links and preserve it on Back. Choosing **Single team** with multiple attached teams must explain the conflict and leave them in place. Enforce the choice atomically on creation, assignment and onboarding, including concurrent approvals. Adding members to the existing team still works.
* **Ecosystems.** Call it **Ecosystem** everywhere in the console; store it as an optional parent link, one level deep. Reject self-parenting, cycles and a third level. Independent products each have a Blueprint. An ecosystem holds only its Foundation (the shared vision) and the overview of its projects: no teams, tasks, kind or milestones of its own. Its overview links to each visible child's health, next milestone and waiting actions with their source dates. Absence of a report reads “No report yet.” Aggregate after checking each child's grants and components. Parentage conveys no permission, task ownership, directive reach or write access.
* **Kind.** Expose the existing Implementation / Research setting. Show the effects before saving a change to a populated project. It changes the landing view, measures and default track for future setup. Preserve existing tracks, items, links and discoverability in browse and export. Record who made the change. Either kind may publish a paper.
* **Reassignment.** Create the operator's independent team directly in its child project during the rebuild. Defer a tool that moves a populated team and its history. Guard the existing assignment route against silently releasing or reinterpreting a populated part, pending proposal or referenced work on cross-project reassignment. Return the dependencies and keep the source intact. Preserve safe assignments and same-project part editing. Today's move releases tracks and the feed joins through current membership. [Blueprint service](../server/src/blueprint/blueprint.ts) · [Work feed](../server/src/admin/workFeed.ts)

## 2. People, grants and agents

The operator grants access to a person. The grant has a **project** or **vault folders** as its scope. A project grant includes Blueprint and its selected components as today. A folder grant carries specific vault paths and no project membership. A person may hold both. Folder grants default to **Read**. The operator may grant **Contribute** so the person can create or revise notes there through the vault's existing version and conflict checks. Apply restricted-folder rules to writes as well as reads. Existing project invitations retain their issued permissions. Keep component dependencies, expiry, two-factor requirements, export permission, decider ceilings and the external board restriction. Evaluate each operation against its target. A permission on one folder cannot combine with a different folder's export or write permission. [Multi-user contract](multi-user.md) · [Policy contracts](authorization.md)

**Agent delegation is part of the person's grant.** A new grant defaults to no delegation. In the same editor the operator may enable team work in named teams of a granted project, or allow an agent to read or contribute in named vault folders. Project work requires the corresponding component and live team membership. Folder delegation can narrow the owner's actions and paths. A Read grant caps every owned agent at Read there. Show the exact permitted operations before save. Explicit download/export, destructive operations and machine use each require the corresponding authority. Owning an agent never confers the operator's admin powers or a human decider vote. A project or vault invitation alone creates no key.

| Scenario | Console | Agent |
|---|---|---|
| Two people build together | Each sees their granted project components. Only an eligible decider sees routed actions. | Each runs their own agents, on the same team or separate teams. Key rules and team role still govern assignment, review and Blueprint writes. |
| A research partner follows papers | Project opens to papers, hypotheses and milestones. Task detail requires the appropriate tasks access. | A delegated coordinator can maintain draft papers in its part. Publication follows the existing approval rule. |
| Two people share a vault folder | A folder-only collaborator lands in Vault, can preview files, revise notes with Contribute and see their access in Account. | A vault-only onboarding request, once approved, issues a bounded key for a teamless agent with a short teamless playbook (no team, no polling). The agent can revise an authorized note with its base revision. There is no team, task board or Blueprint to maintain. Only hub-wide directives reach it. |

**Enforcement contract**

1. Record the owning user on the agent. Manual issuance, onboarding, recruitment, key rotation and checkout/pickup must preserve a verified owner. The operator assigns the owner. A recruited agent inherits its recruiting owner's eligible scope subject to the normal approval and role ceilings. Never infer a new owner from a name, host, grant, file or login. The operator claims his own agents explicitly: the migration that adds owners also adds an audited **Claim all existing agents as mine** step in the console, and owner enforcement switches on only after the operator has run it. Until then agents keep today's rules. Unmapped agents have no collaborator authority.
2. For every concrete operation require a usable owner, a matching current owner delegation, an active session key and its effective policy, the presenting app's active key and applicable cap, and the existing role, resource and safety checks. The operator's active admin ownership adds no project ceiling to his agents, which still need their own permissions. A deny prevails. Validate requested scopes at issuance and evaluate actual targets at use. A base tool can return only permitted data. Scope team rooms, peer recipients, notices, shares, graph sources, jobs and bridge delivery as well as ordinary reads and writes. At publication and delivery check again. Fail closed for an operation with no defined owner-scope mapping.
3. **Work from another owner's agent is trusted only up to that owner's grant.** In a mixed-owner team, task, review and Blueprint text from another owner's agent is labelled with that owner. A recipient must not take an action on its basis that the originating owner's grant would not allow. The hub records the originating owner on the task so the access inspector and the audit can show it. The hub can't see what an agent does in a repository; record that remaining risk in the threat model.
4. Ship the grant-to-operation mapping in the existing policy service and expose its decision in the access inspector. Read-only component access gives no agent write permission. Decider delegation gives no agent approval tool. Narrowing a grant, disabling an owner, account expiry or loss of applicable membership takes effect on the next operation, including work already queued. Reuse the existing release/reassignment paths where a holder becomes ineligible. Audit the agent, owner, target and reason. Account may list the person's agent labels and effective scope. Keep PC details, credential identifiers and secrets in their established admin or local setup surfaces.
5. Derive directive project reach from **all eligible live team memberships** and project IDs. A teamless agent has no project reach. Preserve keep-off as an exclusion from delivery to the agent. For example, a hub directive kept off project A also stays out of the Inbox of an agent shared by A and B. Show those affected agents before the operator saves. Refresh notices and confirmations on membership, grant and scope changes. Update TypeScript and SQL reach together, exercise their parity test, then retire the free-text project label from authorization and forms. Older clients may send a descriptive label during transition. [Directive contract](directives.md) · [SQL reach](../server/src/directives/reachSql.ts)

**Landing and Account.** After the sign-in and two-factor gates, direct a collaborator with exactly one accessible project and one accessible team to that project. With only folder grants, open Vault. Otherwise open member Home with the available destinations. Respect explicit deep links and keep Vault reachable with mixed grants. Account says **Collaborator**, identifies Internal or External partner, lists access, expiry and owned agents, and explains who can change access. An empty Vault uses the full content width and distinguishes an empty shared folder from having no granted folders.

## 3. Setup, research and rebuild

Use the existing onboarding request as the reviewable setup record. Ask where the requirements or research brief come from: an existing source (collect a path or URL), the coordinator (derive them), or the operator (supply them). Show the responsible person or role and the next step in the team view. Existing projects supply their kind. New projects ask for it. A supplied reference is information for the team and carries no permission to fetch it. [Onboarding flow](onboarding.md)

For implementation, file retained requirements under a module. For research, collect lines and their mode, questions or hypotheses, existing **and** planned papers, manuscript reference when available, and their owners. Permit a planned title without a manuscript or claim. Real implementation requirements in a research project still require a valid module. Never coerce an FR under a line. Review the typed records before approval and offer correction for incompatible input. Ask the research coordinator to maintain the papers in the team's existing playbook or standard directive.

Approval provisions the team once. When content was retained it files one linked Blueprint proposal, with each item's intended team or project scope. Use existing proposal review for acceptance. When the only answer is “the operator will supply it,” show that pending responsibility and create no empty proposal. Derive setup progress from the answers and proposal status. Rejected or stale content returns to the named person for correction. A repeated approval after a lost response can locate the original team and proposal. Validate the request version, limits, references and current grants within the provisioning transaction.

**Research workspace.** Put Papers and Hypotheses on the research project's first page. Papers includes planned and published work, status and manuscript where readable. Filter by milestone via `milestone → delivers → paper`. Add one permitted relation, `paper → studies → hypothesis`, for planned coverage. A hypothesis filter includes explicitly linked papers and papers with a claim supported by that hypothesis through an `evidence` link. Deduplicate by paper ID and show the reason for each match. Only evidence contributes to claim support. Extend relation validation, SQL constraints, write rights, rendering, export and import together. The hypothesis page shows its status, criteria, dated changes, referenced experiment tasks and results. A finding belongs to its line and links to a hypothesis through evidence. Sibling findings on a line are not automatically results of that hypothesis. A task is a run only when explicitly identified as such by the team, for example through a task tag and hypothesis reference. Apply task, feed and vault permissions separately to drill-downs. [Item kinds and relations](../server/src/blueprint/kinds.ts) · [Support computation](../server/src/blueprint/support.ts)

**Blueprint restore and write limits.** Keep the readable Markdown export for members. Add a bounded, versioned JSON export for the rebuild, **admin-only**, because it carries agent and team IDs. There is **one** way back in: **restore**, from Markdown or JSON, with a check mode (a dry run) whose plan is the preview. The check plan maps source project, teams, authors, keys, existing IDs, parents and relation endpoints to the intended destination, and reports collisions, unknown references, unsupported versions and unapplied data. Show exactly which items will be created, matched or rejected. Explicitly reconcile any records also proposed during onboarding. Stage a large import as one reviewable change with a base version and digest. Check limits as a whole. Reject or apply it atomically. A retry of the same import returns its prior result. Keep ordinary MCP write limits in force.

Approval records who authorized the import. **Authorship determines how the text may be used.** The **only** trusted source of authorship is the pre-reset backup: restore reads writers from the backed-up database, never from the file, so a rebuilt Blueprint keeps the operator's vision and agreed requirements as his text (L0). Remap verified identities explicitly. Recompute L1/L2 relative to the reader's team from creation and update provenance. Treat unresolved or unverifiable authorship as peer data (L2) and retain any claimed author only as attribution. The current trust helper treats non-agent writers as L0, so implement and test this conservative path before enabling restore from JSON. A JSON field naming an operator, a digest supplied alongside its own file, or an admin clicking Apply is insufficient proof. The trusted platform-doc import remains its own entry point. [Trust model](specification.md#trust-model) · [Blueprint trust implementation](../server/src/blueprint/blueprint.ts)

Use existing immutable item IDs internally where a reference outlives team membership. **MCP clients continue to send and receive keys such as `M14` in the existing call shape.** Resolve the key in the caller's authorized project at write time. `bp_*` calls and `team_ask` refs take an optional `project`. It is required when the caller's live teams span more than one project; otherwise the call is refused, and the refusal lists those projects. Store the event-time project and references when recording task history. Retain display keys for archived items. Resolve parent, link and proposal targets within the destination project, including items declared earlier in the same batch. Compute rights against staged parents and their inherited part. A refused operation refuses the write. An operation requiring a proposal makes the whole batch a proposal. Revalidate on apply. Existing project-qualified links can retain their storage representation while they remain unambiguous. For any pre-existing references that cannot be mapped reliably, report the ambiguity and withhold member-visible history until attributable. Test two projects that both have `M14`, and an agent in teams of both projects calling with and without `project`.

Dropped, waived, cut and archived items release **active-work** capacity. Recheck capacity on reopening and unarchiving as well as creation, including parent and per-kind limits. Maintain the resource ceilings on request size, stored bytes, revisions and import work. This also applies to a 130-item rebuild with dependent children and more papers than the configured paper limit. An excess must identify the applicable limit and leave the project consistent.

## 4. Find work and act on it

**Blueprint browse.** Give each section a stable route, server search, relevant filters, a paged list and a detail view with the actions the viewer may take. Each row identifies key, title, status and parent or team where that distinguishes similar items. The server returns rows, matching total, next cursor and effective filters. Use the same authorized selection for every clickable count and its destination, with a deterministic order and ID tie-breaker. Put filters in the route so Back restores the list. Show an empty result with a way to clear filters. The server derives item actions from the existing rights and kind tables and checks them again on mutation. A browser copy of the permission matrix would drift.

Use domain labels in the kind where they make sense. Requirements opens FRs/NFRs, Acceptance opens ACs, and research opens Lines, Hypotheses or Papers. Prefer exact stored statuses such as Agreed, Built or Testing. Where a grouped **Open** shortcut is retained, define the predicate once by kind and name it in the response. For example, open implementation requirements are draft, agreed or built FRs/NFRs. “Open 12” must return those same twelve IDs over all pages. Project and team cards may count different scopes only if they label and pass that scope.

| View | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Overview | Omit 0/0 Requirements, Acceptance and Milestones. Keep an authorized first-create action discoverable. Foundation has no count. |
| Dropped | Group by the originating section, omit empty groups, and keep dropped entries by default. **Archive** on a dropped entry removes it from ordinary browse, counts and search. Reuse `archived`. Preserve keys, revisions, relationships and a marked export. Preview dependent children and either archive the selected subtree together or require resolution before archiving. Existing report retention continues. |
| Risk | Put likelihood, impact and owner under the title. Keep short Records visible on first open. Long records have an accessible expansion control. |
| Milestone | Lead with its title and description. Show progress only for eligible linked tasks, using the existing task-count rules and viewer scope. |

**Attention words.** Before building, add one short glossary table to [Dashboard UX](dashboard-ux.md): Needs you, For awareness, notices, alerts and the agent Inbox, plus dismissal, acknowledgement and episode. For each, say who sees it and what clears it. Every slice uses only those words.

**Needs you.** Extend the existing server-side needs-you read to support both consoles. Home, team, project and Blueprint Review are filtered entrances to this same list. A row names the source record, version, scope, reason, destination and allowed **resolving actions**. Count distinct source decisions after routing, authorization and filtering, before pagination. Reuse the source workflow for every command. Recheck its version and current decider when acting. On a competing resolution, refresh the row and count together. Preserve keyboard focus on the next item.

| Put in Needs you | Put elsewhere |
|---|---|
| A question for this decider, a proposal awaiting this person's approval, an access request within their ceiling, a human checkpoint or an eligible unblock. | A question awaiting the agent's reply, a submission awaiting its independent agent reviewer, a missed target or failing criterion. Show the responsible party and relevant record in the team or project. |
| The operator's active backlog, submitted onboarding requests, checkout steps he can advance, actionable directive suggestions, feedback triage and vault conflicts. An unclear requirement qualifies when **Clarify** can actually save the clarification and clear its flag. | Informational notices, stalled members, unsupported claims and uncollected keys. Surface in **For awareness** when applicable. A checkout requiring a decision links to that checkout. An informational move notice opens the move or team history. |

Map each source state to its actual action and actor before registering it. “Open,” “Dismiss” or a generic edit permission alone never qualifies a row. Deduplicate alerts about a workflow against that workflow's row. Preserve the existing visibility and acknowledgement of critical security incidents. A viewer's dismissal of an awareness row is separate from an authorized acknowledgement of its underlying alert. Key dismissal by viewer, record and meaningful episode, such as a milestone's due date and missed status. Persist across refresh. A new episode may reappear. Stalled warnings default off per team and missed milestones off per project. Apply settings to every attention surface, including synthetic team badges. Preserve task release, the underlying milestone status and the audit trail.

**Backlog and reviews.** Keep Accept, Discard and Demote admin-only. Add Postpone and Return to active to a pending backlog task. A nullable postponement timestamp suffices. Both moves are versioned and audited. Accept or Discard works from either list and clears postponement. Demotion starts active. Postponed work stays pending, outside Home and Needs you, and cannot be assigned. Combine the project and tag filters with Active / Postponed before paging and counting. Keep relayed, already-open tasks in their existing group. Render Markdown in the brief and context. Preserve the plain-text `short_summary` contract and existing fallback. Show the entire summary if it fits four rendered lines, expandable otherwise, with the full text in task detail. [Task lifecycle](specification.md#task-lifecycle) · [Summary implementation](../server/src/teams/shortSummary.ts)

For a waiting review show task, submitted version, reviewer, time waiting and the existing handoff text. Record **seen** only on that holder's successful full read of the specific task or successful action on its current turn. A list fetch, unrelated tool call or failed attempt leaves it unseen. Seen, started and reviewed are distinct. Human checkpoints continue to route to their authorized decider. The normal independent reviewer retains the review. [Task turns](../server/src/teams/turns.ts)

**Console load.** Capture requests and payload bytes for sign-in and steady-state Home, team, Blueprint and Topology on the seeded local hub. Read viewer identity once per session as appropriate, refresh common counts together, load the selected view's data, stop its poll on exit, and refresh affected data after an action. Topology's timer refreshes Topology. Compare like-for-like traces in review and verify that reduced polling still reflects grant revocation and resolved decisions.

## 5. Read notes, media and agent messages

Use the existing Markdown renderer for supported formatting. Give each field a documented rendering context: plain labels and task summaries, inline text on compact rows, or full documents for criteria, briefs, records and playbooks. Escape untrusted text and enforce the permitted link schemes in every context. Render operator and collaborator vault notes with the same resolver. Resolve relative paths from the containing note, vault-root paths from the vault, and wiki links against the visible index. Ambiguous names offer only visible matches. Preserve fragment anchors. Represent unavailable targets as unavailable without disclosing whether a hidden file exists. Replace placeholder links with a real scoped destination wherever the hub knows the path.

**Vault media.** Use one authenticated byte-serving path for the admin and member views. Authorize the canonical file and requested representation on every full or partial request, including metadata and resume. Use the vault's path containment and file checks. Bind reads to the verified file/revision. Listing a folder gives no implicit permission on a clip. Apply restricted-folder rules and audit external previews and downloads with the existing semantics. Inline playback delivers the original bytes to the browser. Require the folder's export permission for originals, whether requested as an attachment or for playback. Explain that requirement beside an unavailable player. Text previews continue under the existing preview permission.

Serve only recognized raster image and supported audio formats inline after verifying content type from the bytes. Reuse the player in folder rows and note embeds. Support bounded single byte ranges with correct 200, 206 and 416 responses, MIME type and lengths. Deny a revoked request before sending bytes or size. Use private, non-shared caching, `nosniff`, same-origin resource policy and a sandboxed content security policy. Serve SVG, HTML, PDF and unrecognized content only as authorized attachments. An unavailable or unsupported clip should leave its note readable. Keep video outside this delivery. [Vault contract](vault.md) · [Existing console vault API](../server/src/admin/vaultApi.ts)

**Agent page.** Keep Tell the agent as the existing compose action. Put hub and operator notices in a dedicated Inbox, with sender, time, subject and existing acknowledgement state. Activity continues to describe what happened. Opening a console card never acknowledges a notice for its agent. Show the complete team playbook inline when short and offer a keyboard-accessible expansion for the full long text. Reuse the card registry and preserve useful focus and reading order at phone widths.

## 6. Hardware

Deliver Hardware last, as a sidebar destination for the operator. Reuse the PC's existing machine identity with an optional compute role. A machine may offer compute without hosting an agent or bridge. Assisted registration lets the operator select or add a machine, record its SSH alias or access instructions, timezone and availability windows, set memory/CPU/GPU budgets, then preview what eligible agents will see. Credentials stay on the machine or in the user's existing SSH setup. The hub never opens SSH, installs keys, starts jobs or measures host usage.

**This release coordinates; it does not schedule.** An authorized agent with a live task it can work on joins a machine's ordered waiting list, and leaves it when done or cancelled. One holder per machine at a time. The holder reports its task, estimate and progress; the hub records what it is told. Memory, CPU and GPU are operator-declared shared-use limits shown on joining. Label availability as scheduled and use as reported.

Show who holds the machine, task, estimated finish, last report, and waiting order. Release or cancel moves the next entry up. Losing the agent's grant or task removes its entry and flags an active holder for the operator. A waiting entry never grants access to the machine, SSH credentials or other teams' task details.

**Deferred until the operator has used the simple version:** daily time allowances and local-day accounting, enforced claims and offer expiry, the "confirmation needed" state and operator clearance, and a machine allowance in the owner's delegation.
